Assignment 3, step by step: the geometallurgical comminution test and its report

What they ask

The laboratory instruction is written out in full on the Canvas page "Laboratory — geometallurgical comminution test (full instruction)" of module M6, and the Canvas assignment page "Assignment 3 — GCT laboratory report" says what to hand in and what a pass requires. Two attachments (the original instruction with the sieving protocol and the RRSB diagram, and the article Mwanga, Rosenkranz and Lamberg 2017) were still locked for students on 10 September; the page carries everything needed. Group hand-in: one PDF report plus the Excel file with the data and the calculations. Attendance is compulsory. Due Sunday 4 October at 23:59 as Canvas reads it on 9 September (the course said assignment dates may still be updated). Point by point:

1. Purpose. To train the conduct of a small-scale comminution test, the geometallurgical comminution test (GCT), for estimating the Bond work index from sample sizes small enough to come from drill core. The test was developed at this university and has been compared with the traditional method with satisfactory results. By the end you should run the test, evaluate the data, calculate a work index from your own energy and size measurements, and say how far the answer can be trusted.

2. The material and the equipment. Feed samples from Swedish ore deposits, prepared, crushed and ready to split; 220 g of feed per test; the sieve series of the protocol 1680, 883, 417, 212, 106 and 75 µm, with the material passing 75 µm collected as the last fraction; mass in and mass out recorded at every sieving, and the loss. A riffle splitter, a mechanical sieve shaker, a balance, the small laboratory ball mill of the GCT, and the wall-mounted energy meter.

3. Safety and rules. The safety instruction from the responsible laboratory engineer, signed, before entering; laboratory coat, safety glasses, ear protection and a dust mask when needed; long hair tied back; the instructions of the engineer and the supervisor at all times; the instruction read before the class; and each group presents a plan, orally, to the assistant (who does what, in what order, what is recorded at each step). A missing or inconsistent plan sends the whole group back.

4. The procedure. Split the sample with the riffler down to a 220 g feed (splitting, not scooping). Size the feed by dry sieving on the series above and calculate the cumulative per cent finer. Read the energy meter before the first grinding interval. Grind in successive, cumulative intervals: after each, read and record the energy consumed and sieve the mill product; keep the sieved material, recombine it and return it to the mill for the next interval. The grinding times in the instruction are 2, 5, 8, 11 and 25 minutes (the published method uses cumulative times of 2, 5, 10, 17 and 25; follow the supervisor and record which schedule you used). Leave with, per interval, the grinding time, the meter readings before and after, and a complete sieve protocol: six size analyses in all, the feed plus five products.

5. The evaluation. Each size distribution on the RRSB diagram: the 80 % passing size of the feed, x80,F, and of each product, x80,P, with the parameters d63.2 and n. The specific surface area from the diagram's marginal scale, assuming spherical particles (state it). The meter reads gross electrical energy: correct it with the drive efficiency η = 64 %, divide by the sample mass, cumulative from the start, in kWh/t. Plot the specific energy against the product 80 % size and then as W against 1/x80,P, which should be a straight line. Back-calculate the work index for every interval from Bond's law, and take the GCT work index from the slope k of the line, Wi,GCT=k/10, reporting slope, intercept and coefficient of determination. The GCT index is systematically higher than the standard Bond index; the published conversion uses η = 0.64, a scaling factor β = 2.65 between the small mill and the Bond mill and a correlation factor of 4.0; take its exact form from equation 5 of the article and confirm it with the supervisor.

6. The report (assignment page). A cover page with the names, the laboratory date and the title; a short introduction with the purpose of the GCT; a methodology section (the procedure actually followed, the theory of the work index by the GCT method, the samples, the equipment); raw data in appendices and the graphs in the body; the results (size distributions, the energy-size diagram, the work index per interval, error sources); a comparison with the other groups; a conclusion. PDF report plus the Excel with all data and the numerical evaluation.

7. What a pass requires. All sections present, the method described well enough that another group could repeat the run; feed and product distributions with the sieving protocol filled in and the mass balance closing, losses reported rather than absorbed; the RRSB evaluation with F80, P80, d63.2 and n per interval; energy corrected for drive losses and plotted against product size; a work index per interval, not one number, with a comment on how it varies; error sources identified and discussed (sampling, sieving, the energy reading, the assumptions of the RRSB fit), concrete ones from your own session; an explicit comparison with at least one other group's work index with an attempt to explain the difference.

Note

The course's rules apply to the report: the measurements, the evaluation and the discussion are the group's own, and the assistant approves the plan before anything is ground. This guide explains what the sheet asks, how the session runs and how the evaluation is checked; it does not contain any result.

How the work flows

The assignment is a plan, a session of five cumulative intervals, an evaluation that must come out as a straight line, and a report that another group could repeat. Nothing is designed; what you turn is the grinding time, and what you read is energy and size. Four things have different roles:

  • Fixed for every run: the feed as prepared (220 g after splitting), the sieve series, the mill and the meter, the drive efficiency of 64 %, and the interval schedule you agreed with the supervisor and wrote down.
  • Chosen once, before the session: the plan (roles, order, what is recorded where), the protocol sheets ready with a column for the meter reading before and after, and the answer to Pre-lab Q6.
  • Turned in the laboratory: only the cumulative grinding time, five times. Everything else is measured: the meter before and after each interval, the mass of every sieve fraction, the loss at every sieving.
  • Read in the evaluation: the six size analyses on the RRSB diagram (the 80 % sizes, d63.2 and n), the five cumulative specific energies, the line of W against 1/x80,P with its slope, intercept and R2, and one work index per interval from Bond's law.
  • Compared at the end: the work index per interval against the slope value, the GCT index against a Bond index through the article's conversion, and your group's index against at least one other group's.

In the sheet's words, "successive grinding intervals" means cumulative on the same sample, so the sixth analysis is of material ground for the total time; "this should be a straight line" is the test of the method, and points that fall off it at the start are a finding to report, not an error; "a work index for each interval" means five numbers plus the slope, never one average.

How to check the evaluation

  • The mass balance is the first result. At every sieving, mass in minus mass out is a loss; report it per analysis. A loss you cannot account for is a finding, and a protocol whose masses do not add up comes back for revision.
  • Sizes come from the diagram, not from a guess. Each size analysis is plotted on the RRSB paper (a Rosin-Rammler distribution is a straight line there); the 80 % passing size and the parameters d63.2 and n are read from it and reported per interval. The pass criteria say "derived from the diagram rather than asserted".
  • Energy on one basis. Specific energy is the meter difference times η = 0.64 over the sample mass, in kWh/t, cumulative from the first interval; the same 220 g (minus recorded losses) all the way. A meter reading used without the efficiency, or an energy per interval where a cumulative one is needed, moves the whole line.
  • The line is the test. Plot W against 1/x80,P and fit a straight line: the slope k gives Wi,GCT=k/10, the intercept is negative and read as an infinitely large starting size, and R2 is the evidence that the linear model is allowed. If the first one or two points fall off, say so; some ores become linear only after an initial phase.
  • Bond's law, per interval. With W in kWh/t and the sizes in µm, W=Wi(10x80,P−10x80,F), so Wi=W10x80,P−10x80,F for each interval. Report all five and comment on how they vary; the variation is part of the answer.
  • The conversion to a Bond index. The GCT index runs systematically high; the article's equation 5 converts it with η = 0.64, β = 2.65 and the factor 4.0. State the form you used in the methodology and confirm it with the supervisor; do not present the GCT index as a Bond index.

Key points

  1. The plan is graded before the mill turns. Roles, order, what is recorded at every step, on paper, presented orally; the group without a consistent plan is sent back. Pre-lab Q6 must be submitted first.
  2. Six analyses, five energies, one line. The feed plus five cumulative products; the meter before and after each interval; the line of W against 1/x80,P with slope, intercept and R2. The report is built from those numbers and from nothing else.
  3. Losses are results. Mass in and out at every sieving, and the loss reported, never absorbed into a fraction.
  4. A work index per interval, plus the slope. Five Bond back-calculations, a comment on their variation, and the GCT index from the slope; then the conversion to a Bond index with the article's equation, stated.
  5. Error sources from your own session. Sampling (the split), sieving (losses, blinding, time), the energy reading (resolution, the moment of reading), the RRSB fit (points off the line). Generic lists do not pass; concrete ones from your run do.
  6. The comparison is compulsory. At least one other group's work index, and an attempt to explain the difference (a different schedule, a different feed, a loss, a reading).
  7. What it feeds. The work index is the hardness proxy the GeoMet project's block model runs on, and seminar theme 2 opens with how it was estimated.

How to start, step by step

1

Read the full instruction, agree the plan, do Pre-lab Q6

ClickThe Canvas page of the laboratory, end to end. On one page: who splits, who sieves, who reads the meter, who writes; the order of the steps; the interval schedule you will propose (2, 5, 8, 11, 25 min); a sieve protocol per analysis with the six sieves and the pan, a column for mass in, mass out and loss; a column for the meter reading before and after each interval. Submit Pre-lab Q6.
ExpectA written plan you can present orally in two minutes, and the protocol sheets ready before you enter.
WhyThe assistant approves the plan or sends the group back; the pass criteria start with "the laboratory attended and carried out with a plan approved by the assistant".
Sessions are by project group (A and B on Thursday 10 September at 13:00, C and D on Wednesday 16 September at 08:15 in the announcement); know which you are in, and bring the safety instruction signed.
2

Split to 220 g with the riffler, and record what you did

ClickDivide the prepared material with the riffle splitter down to the 220 g feed; note the number of splits and the mass you kept. Never scoop.
ExpectA feed mass close to 220 g, written down, and a sentence in the report on how it was obtained.
WhyThe split is the first place a bias can enter, and the report asks about error sources.
Keep the rejects until the session is over, in case a sample is lost.
3

Size the feed by dry sieving

ClickSieve the feed on 1680, 883, 417, 212, 106 and 75 µm with the pan for the −75 µm; weigh every fraction; record mass in, mass out and the loss; calculate the cumulative per cent finer.
ExpectThe first of six protocols, with the mass balance closing within a loss you have written down.
WhyThe feed's 80 % size, x80,F, enters every work index.
Sieving time and blinding change the result: use the same time for every analysis and note it.
4

Read the meter, grind the first interval, read it again

ClickNote the wall meter before starting; grind for the first cumulative time; note the meter after. Record both readings and the time.
ExpectThe first energy difference, in the meter's unit, next to the interval time.
WhyEvery later reading is only useful as a difference from the one before.
Read the meter at the same moment relative to the mill stopping, every time; write the unit of the meter down.
5

Sieve the product, recombine, and repeat for the remaining intervals

ClickSieve the mill product on the same series, weigh every fraction, record the loss; recombine all fractions and return them to the mill; grind to the next cumulative time; read the meter; sieve again. Five intervals in all.
ExpectFive product protocols and five energy differences, with the losses of every sieving recorded.
WhyThe intervals are cumulative on the same sample; the sixth analysis describes material ground for the total time.
If the schedule on the day differs from 2, 5, 8, 11, 25 min (the article uses 2, 5, 10, 17, 25), follow the supervisor and record which you used.
6

Evaluate the sizes on the RRSB diagram

ClickPlot each of the six analyses on the RRSB paper; read the 80 % passing size, $d_{63.2}$ and $n$ for each; estimate the specific surface area from the marginal scale, stating the spherical-particle assumption.
ExpectA table with six rows: x80, d63.2, n and the surface area per analysis.
WhyThe pass criteria want these derived from the diagram, per interval.
A poor straight line on the RRSB paper is worth a sentence: it says the distribution is not Rosin-Rammler over the whole range.
7

Energy on one basis, and the line

ClickFor each interval: specific energy = meter difference × 0.64 / sample mass, cumulative, in kWh/t. Plot $W$ against $x_{80,P}$, then $W$ against $1/\sqrt{x_{80,P}}$; fit the straight line; report slope, intercept and $R^2$; $W_{i,\mathrm{GCT}} = k/10$.
ExpectTwo diagrams and one work index from the slope, with its statistics.
WhyThe straight line is the test of the method and the GCT index is its slope.
An intercept that is positive, or an R2 far from 1, is a finding about your run, not something to hide.
8

A work index per interval, and the conversion to a Bond index

ClickFor each of the five intervals, back-calculate $W_i$ from Bond's law with $W$ in kWh/t and the sizes in µm; comment on how the five values vary. Convert the GCT index to an estimated Bond index with the article's equation 5 ($\eta$ = 0.64, $\beta$ = 2.65, factor 4.0), stating the form you used and confirming it with the supervisor.
ExpectFive interval values, the slope value, and one estimated Bond work index with the equation written out.
Why"A work index back-calculated for each grinding interval, not a single number for the whole test" is a pass criterion.
Sizes in millimetres in Bond's law give an index off by a factor of about 30.
9

Error sources from your own session, and the comparison with another group

ClickList the concrete ones: how the split was done, sieving times and losses per analysis, the resolution and timing of the meter reading, points off the line and why. Then compare your GCT index with at least one other group's and try to explain the difference (schedule, feed, losses, readings).
ExpectA discussion with names and numbers from your run, and one comparison with an explanation attempted.
WhyBoth are pass criteria, and a report without the comparison comes back for revision.
Generic error sources ("human error") do not pass; "the 106 µm sieve lost 1.3 g in the third analysis" does.
10

Write the report and attach the spreadsheet

ClickCover; introduction and purpose; methodology (procedure actually followed, theory of the GCT work index, samples, equipment); results with the graphs in the body and the raw data in appendices; error sources; the comparison; a conclusion. PDF in Canvas, with the Excel of all data and the numerical evaluation alongside.
ExpectA report another group could repeat the run from, and a spreadsheet from which every number in it can be recomputed.
Why"Reproducible from the attached spreadsheet" is how the pass is judged.
One report per group, everyone's contribution visible; the due date is the one Canvas shows the week you submit.

Deliverables checklist

Common mistakes

  • Arriving without a written plan, or with one that does not say who records what: the group is sent back.
  • Scooping instead of splitting, and not saying how the 220 g was obtained.
  • Losses at sieving absorbed into a fraction instead of reported; a mass balance that does not close.
  • Energies per interval where the method needs cumulative ones, or the meter reading used without the 64 % drive efficiency.
  • The 80 % sizes read off a table instead of the RRSB diagram, or Bond's law with sizes in millimetres.
  • One average work index instead of one per interval plus the slope; the GCT index presented as a Bond index without the conversion.
  • Error sources listed generically; no comparison with another group.
