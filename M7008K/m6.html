Geometallurgical test methods

A geometallurgical model is only as good as the measurements behind it, and measurements cost money and sample. The central design problem of geometallurgy is that the responses you actually care about — throughput, recovery, concentrate grade — can be measured properly only on large samples in slow, expensive tests, while the model needs numbers in every block of the deposit.

GCT laboratory report

Due: w40 derived

What a pass requires
  1. All sections present, with the method described well enough that another group could repeat your run
  2. Feed and product size distributions reported, with the sieving protocol filled in and the mass balance closing: losses reported rather than quietly absorbed
  3. The RRSB evaluation carried out, with F80, P80, d63.2 and n given for each interval
  4. Energy corrected for drive losses, and the specific energy against product size plotted
  5. A work index back-calculated for each grinding interval, not a single number for the whole test, with a comment on how it varies across the intervals
  6. Error sources identified and discussed (sampling, sieving, energy reading, the assumptions in the RRSB fit)
  7. An explicit comparison with at least one other group's work index, with an attempt to explain any difference
