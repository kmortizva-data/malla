Module 1 · w36

The geometallurgical approach

A mineral resource is estimated from more than ten thousand chemical assays. The plant that will treat that resource for the next twenty years is usually designed on five to ten metallurgical samples. Everything this course is about follows from that asymmetry: the deposit varies, the model does not know it varies, and the plant finds out the hard way.

quiz 10 pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 2 · w36, w37

Sampling and sampling theory

Every number you will use for the rest of this course — every assay, every mineral grade, every work index — is a measurement made on a few hundred grams that stand in for a few million tonnes. If that stand-in is wrong, nothing downstream can repair it: the most careful mineralogy in the world on a biased sample gives a confident wrong answer.

quiz 10 pts0/9 checkpoints
Module 3 · w37

Characterisation methods and tools

M1 told you a geometallurgical model needs mineral grades, textures and hardness, and M2 told you how to get a defensible sample. This module is about the instruments in between — and about one habit that separates a useful characterisation programme from an expensive one. The habit is to start from the question, not from the instrument.

quiz 10 pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 4 · w38

Element-to-mineral conversion

A concentrator separates minerals . A drill core is described by elements , because chemical assays are cheap enough to run on every metre of every hole while quantitative mineralogy is not. A geometallurgical block model that carries only grades therefore carries the wrong variable.

quiz 10 pts · assignment None pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 5 · w38

Particle behaviour and liberation

M4 gave you minerals instead of elements. This module takes one more step, and it is the step that makes the rest of the course work: a flotation cell, a magnetic separator or a spiral does not see minerals either. It sees particles — fragments of rock, each one made of a mixture of minerals in some proportion — and it acts on the properties of the whole particle.

quiz 10 pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 6 · w39

Geometallurgical test methods

A geometallurgical model is only as good as the measurements behind it, and measurements cost money and sample. The central design problem of geometallurgy is that the responses you actually care about — throughput, recovery, concentrate grade — can be measured properly only on large samples in slow, expensive tests, while the model needs numbers in every block of the deposit.

quiz 10 pts · assignment None pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 7 · w39

Modelling, simulation and process metallurgy

Everything you have done so far produces numbers about rock: assays, modal mineralogy, liberation, a work index, a recovery from a bench test. This module is where those numbers are finally made to do something. A flowsheet model turns ore properties into plant performance — tonnes per hour, kilowatt-hours per tonne, per cent recovery, per cent concentrate grade — one ore domain at a time.

assignment None pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 8 · w41

Economic appraisal

A block of rock is not ore because of what it contains. It is ore because someone can make money out of it, and "someone can make money out of it" is a calculation with a definite answer. This module is that calculation: what the payable metal is worth, what is deducted before you see any of it, what it costs to mine and mill, and where the line falls between ore and waste.

quiz 10 pts0/8 checkpoints
Module 9 · w37, w38, w39, w40, w41

The GeoMet game

Your group runs a company on an iron ore deposit inside an online application: borrow, drill, sample, order analyses, build, mine and settle, turn by turn, through weeks 37 to 41. Information costs money and time.

quiz 10 pts
Module 10 · w40, w42

Project, study trip and seminar

The spine of the course: the GeoMet project (the game is the project), the compulsory study trip in week 40, and the final seminar in week 42 that sets the course grade: a 20-minute group presentation and 10 minutes of questions, each student scored on three criteria.
