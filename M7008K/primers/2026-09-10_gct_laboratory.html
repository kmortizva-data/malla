Before the GCT laboratory: a work index from 220 grams

What this session is

The geometallurgical comminution test (GCT) in the mineral processing laboratory: compulsory, and handed in as Assignment 3, one report per group (due Sunday 4 October in Canvas as read on 9 September; the course said dates may still move). The announcement of 9 September splits the class in two: Thursday 10 September at 13:00 for project groups A and B, Wednesday 16 September at 08:15 for groups C and D. TimeEdit shows the Thursday slots as "Project" in F441a and the Monday and Wednesday laborations with Sina Shakibania in F433, so follow the announcement and check which group you are in. Before you enter: the safety instruction signed, laboratory coat, safety glasses, ear protection and a dust mask when needed, long hair tied back. Each group presents its plan orally to the assistant; a missing or inconsistent plan sends the whole group back. Pre-lab Q6 must be submitted first.

Three ideas to arrive with

  1. Why a test this small exists. A geometallurgical programme needs a hardness number for hundreds of samples, and the standard Bond test needs far more material and time than any drill programme can supply. The GCT, developed at this university, estimates the Bond work index from 220 g of feed, and has been compared with the standard method with satisfactory results. The report's introduction is this paragraph in your words.
  2. The measurement is energy against size. Split (never scoop) the prepared material down to 220 g; sieve the feed on 1680, 883, 417, 212, 106 and 75 µm; read the wall energy meter; grind in cumulative intervals (2, 5, 8, 11 and 25 minutes in the instruction; the published method uses 2, 5, 10, 17, 25; follow the supervisor and write down which); after each interval read the meter, sieve the product, recombine it and return it to the mill. You leave with six size analyses and five energy differences, and with every mass loss written down, because a loss you cannot account for is a result.
  3. The evaluation is a straight line. Each size analysis goes on the RRSB diagram to read the 80 % passing size. The specific energy is the meter reading corrected by the drive efficiency, 64 %, divided by the sample mass, in kWh/t and cumulative. Plot it against one over the square root of the product's 80 % size: the slope divided by ten is the GCT work index, and Bond's law back-calculates a work index for every interval besides. The GCT index runs systematically higher than a Bond index; the article's equation 5 converts it (efficiency 0.64, scaling factor 2.65, correlation factor 4.0), and the supervisor confirms which form to use.

Two questions to keep in mind

  • Where can a bias enter before the mill starts, and how will the report account for the mass we lose at each sieving?
  • Do our first one or two points fall on the line, and if not, is that an error or the ore becoming linear only after an initial phase?

Five terms

TermIn plain words
Work indexThe energy, in kWh/t, to take a tonne from very coarse to 80 % passing 100 µm; the ore's hardness number for grinding
80 % passing sizeThe size that 80 % of the mass is finer than; the one point of a distribution Bond's law uses
RRSB diagramPaper on which a Rosin-Rammler distribution is a straight line; gives the 80 % size and the parameters d63.2 and n
Specific energyEnergy per tonne of sample, cumulative from the first interval, after the 64 % drive efficiency
Riffle splitterThe device that divides a sample into halves without letting anyone choose which grains go where

Where it lands in the assignment

Assignment 3 is the report of this laboratory: a cover with the group's names and the date; an introduction with the purpose of the GCT and why a small-scale test is needed; a methodology section with the procedure you actually followed, the theory of the work index by the GCT method, the samples and the equipment; the raw data in appendices (sieve protocols, energy readings, times) and the graphs in the body; the results, with the size distributions, the energy-size diagram, the work index per interval and from the slope with its intercept and coefficient of determination, and a discussion of the error sources. The hardness number you produce is the kind of proxy the project's block model runs on, and seminar theme 2 (ore processing) opens with how the Bond work index was estimated.
