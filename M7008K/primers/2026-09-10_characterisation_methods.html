Three ideas to arrive with

  1. Start from the question, not from the instrument. The course sorts characterisation questions into seven kinds: which minerals are present; structure, texture and grain size; the chemical composition of one mineral; how much of each mineral (modal mineralogy); surface properties; particle size; and the composition of particles, which is liberation. "Which minerals" and "how much of each" are different questions answered by different tools, and neither says how the minerals are intergrown.
  2. Identification is cheap, quantification is expensive. Qualitative data say a phase is there; quantitative data say how much, with an uncertainty. Optical microscopy quantifies to about ±5 % and depends on the operator; automated SEM mineralogy quantifies but cannot tell magnetite from hematite; XRD names crystalline phases but destroys the texture and misses anything below about 0.5 to 5 %; an assay gives elements, never minerals. Reading an identification as if it were a quantification is the classic error in reports.
  3. A programme is a hierarchy, not a single method. Identify first (microscopy, XRD), because you cannot quantify a phase you have not named; quantify second; put the expensive method on ten chosen samples rather than on all of them. Plan backwards from the decision the number has to support.

Two questions to keep in mind

  • For the decision in front of me, which method, on how many samples, and what would it fail to see?
  • Is this number in the report a count or an identification, and would the method that produced it have separated magnetite from hematite?

Five terms

TermIn plain words
Modal mineralogyHow much of each mineral a sample contains, by mass or volume; the variable the process depends on
TextureHow the mineral grains sit against each other: sizes, shapes, intergrowths; what decides liberation
Detection limitThe smallest amount a method can see; below it a phase is invisible, not absent
StereologyA polished section is two-dimensional; the particle is three-dimensional; a 2D cut misreads locking
Elemental deportmentWhich mineral an element sits in, and how much of it in each

Where it lands in the assignment

Warm-up Q3, which must be submitted, matches questions to instruments. In the GeoMet game every analysis you order costs money and game time, so the choice is by question, not by acronym. In the project report's methods section and in seminar theme 1 (was there enough sampling and analysis, and what does the dataset support) the limits of each method are the argument.
