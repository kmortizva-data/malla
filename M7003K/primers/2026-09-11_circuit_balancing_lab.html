What this session is

Friday 11 September, two computer-lab slots in F441a (13:00 to 14:30 and 14:45 to 16:15), Mehdi Parian. Folder 04 Circuit balancing holds the instructions (Lab_CircuitBalancing_MP.pdf, Pålsson and Parian), the theory (CircuitBalancing_MP.pdf), an exercise sheet (Ex_CircuitBalancing.pdf) and the Excel workbooks Circuit1, Circuit3, Mineral and Product. This is the mandatory laboratory class of examination module 0003; the report is due about a week after the class and is the whole group's responsibility.

Three ideas to arrive with

  1. A sampled circuit never balances by itself. A rod mill and a pebble mill grinding circuit was sampled: rod mill feed 38.5 t/h, pebbles 9.7 t/h, middlings returned from the Cu-Pb flotation 13.1 t/h, and assays of Cu, Zn, Pb and S on seven streams (feed, pebbles, pebble mill discharge, middlings, cyclone feed, underflow, overflow). Assays carry errors, so the raw numbers violate the mass balance at every node. Balancing means finding the smallest adjustments to the measurements that make every node close, weighting each measurement by how much you trust it.
  1. Trust is an input you must write down. The sheet spells it out: the rod mill feed rate is known with high precision; the pebble feed rate is known well but its grades were not analysed (assume the same as the rod mill feed); the middlings are known by grade but the flow is only inferred; sulphur and lead analyses are worse than the rest. In HSC Sim these become the standard deviations of the input file. A balance that ignores them gives negative flows or zero streams, which is exactly what task (c) tells you to avoid.
  1. Elements in, minerals out. The note in bold on the sheet: calculate mineral grades from the elements first. Cu is carried by chalcopyrite (CuFeS₂, 34.6 % Cu), Zn by sphalerite (ZnS, 67.1 % Zn), Pb by galena (PbS, 86.6 % Pb), and the sulphur left after those three is pyrite (FeS₂, 53.4 % S); what remains is gangue. The Mineral.xls workbook does the conversion. Toy check on the rod mill feed: 1.09 % Cu → 3.15 % chalcopyrite; 2.57 % Zn → 3.83 % sphalerite; 0.26 % Pb → 0.30 % galena; those three carry about 1.1 + 1.3 + 0.04 = 2.4 % of the 28.0 % S, so about 25.6 % S is left for pyrite, that is roughly 48 % pyrite. Two-thirds of that "ore" is gangue.

Two questions to keep in mind

  • Task (d) asks for the circulating load of each mineral as a function of its density, gangue included, on one diagram. Before the lab, predict the shape: galena (7.5 g/cm³) against pyrite (5.0) against sphalerite (4.1) against chalcopyrite (4.2) against gangue (2.7). What does a rising line say about how the hydrocyclone classifies dense minerals?
  • Why does the sheet want a simplified block diagram with numbered nodes and streams before anything is typed? (Hint: the input file is nothing but that numbering.)

Five terms

TermIn one line
Mass balance nodeA point where streams meet or split; in and out must be equal for every component
Data reconciliationAdjusting measured values, weighted by their uncertainty, until every node balances
Standard deviation as weightHow HSC Sim knows which numbers to move least: the precise ones
Circulating loadMass returned to the mill divided by the fresh feed; here computed per mineral
Mineral conversionElement assays turned into mineral grades through stoichiometry (34.6 % Cu in chalcopyrite, and so on)

Where it lands in the assignment

The lab report (introduction, methods, analyses, results and discussion, plus protocols, tables and diagrams as a ZIP) is graded U/G# inside examination module 0003 together with the assignments. The same balancing arithmetic returns in Assignment 2 (product and water balances) and in every flowsheet question of the written exam.

Bring: the block diagram already drawn on paper with your numbering, the mineral conversion of the rod mill feed done by hand (so you can check the workbook), and a copy of Circuit1.xls opened once so the lab time goes to balancing, not to finding files. Open the material folder from the module page.
