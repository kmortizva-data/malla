What this session is

Tuesday 8 September, 10:15 to 11:45, room E239, lecture by Olof Martinsson. In the course material it is folder 03 Geology - Sulphides and the slide deck F1_Sulphide_deposits.pdf (on your machine under the module's material). It is the geologist's half-day for processing engineers: where the sulphide ores you will treat in modules 5 to 7 come from, and why their textures decide how they have to be processed.

Three ideas to arrive with

  1. An ore is a mineral deposit that pays. Every ore is a local enrichment of one or more minerals, but not every mineral deposit is an ore. The Baltic Shield around Luleå (arc-related volcanic and sedimentary rocks accreted to the Archean craton) is the most important mining area in the EU, with Fe, Cu, Zn, Pb, Ag, Au, Ti, Cr, Ni, Te and more. Kiruna, Malmberget, Aitik, Garpenberg, Kevitsa: the plants of this course sit on this map.
  1. Three ways to concentrate metals, and each leaves a fingerprint. Orthomagmatic deposits crystallise from a melt (mafic and ultramafic rocks: Ni, Cu, PGE, Cr, Ti, V, Fe); hydrothermal fluids at 100 to 600 °C and high salinity precipitate Cu, Zn, Pb, Ag, Au, Fe, Mn, U, Mo, W, Sn; meteoric water at surface temperature gives Fe and Mn sediments and weathering enrichments (Ni, Al, Cu), while placers concentrate the weathering-resistant minerals (Au, Ti, Zr). The lecture's own summary of the chain: source (mantle or crust), extraction (leaching or partial melting), transport (fluids or melt), deposition (precipitation of ore minerals).
  1. Texture is a processing parameter. How the ore minerals were deposited (infilling of open spaces, replacement, chemical sediment) controls whether the ore is massive (more than 50 % ore minerals), banded, disseminated or in veins. That is the geologist's way of saying liberation size and grade: a massive iron ore at 65 % Fe gives 850 kg of magnetite concentrate per tonne mined; a disseminated copper ore at 0.3 % Cu gives 10 kg of chalcopyrite concentrate per tonne. Same tonne through the crusher, a ratio of 85 in what comes out.

Two questions to keep in mind

  • Magmatic Ni-Cu (Norilsk, Kevitsa) forms when a sulphide melt separates from the magma and its droplets sink to the base of the intrusion: massive ore below, disseminated above, pyrrhotite-dominated with pentlandite and chalcopyrite. What does a pyrrhotite-dominated ore mean for flotation and for the sulphur balance downstream?
  • The lecture lists an internal against an external sulphur source (high Ni-Cu in the sulphides against moderate to low). Why would a processing engineer care where the sulphur came from?

Five terms

TermIn one line
Baltic (Fennoscandian) ShieldThe Precambrian bedrock of Sweden, Finland and Norway; the EU's main mining region
Orthomagmatic depositOre minerals crystallised directly from a magma, disseminated, as cumulate layers or as segregations
Hydrothermal depositOre minerals precipitated from hot, saline fluids: the origin of most Cu, Zn, Pb, Ag and Au ores
Disseminated / massive / banded / veinThe four textures the lecture uses; they set liberation size, grade and the flowsheet
Pyrrhotite, pentlandite, chalcopyriteThe mineral trio of magmatic Ni-Cu ores: the iron sulphide host, the nickel carrier, the copper carrier

Where it lands in the assignment

Period 1 ends with a written exam on the metal-ore topics, and old exams (folder Old exams, 2014 to 2022 with one suggested solution) show that ore type, mineralogy and the reasons behind a flowsheet are asked together. The three period 1 assignments (product balance, limiting grades and selectivity, mass and water balance) are arithmetic on exactly the ores this lecture describes: the Cu, Pb-Zn and iron ore cases of modules 6, 7 and 10.

Bring: a look at the map on slide 2 (which of the dots have you already seen in the Simulation course? Kiruna, Aitik), and the question of what a complex sulphide ore is going to mean for selective flotation in module 7.
