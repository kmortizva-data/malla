Why this module exists

Mineral Processing at Luleå is not one course but four examinations wearing one code, and the first thing to understand is which piece of work feeds which one. The introduction lecture of 1 September 2026 lays out the aim, the two study periods, the four graded modules, the teachers and the trips; the syllabus and the formula compilation in the same folder are the rulebook and the toolbox. This short reading turns them into a map: where the lessons of Malla sit in the course, what the written exam of period one actually tests, and which habits from day one pay off in October.

The ideas

1. What the course promises, in the university's words

The aim is an opportunity to understand mineral processes for ores, industrial minerals, recycling products and mineral fuels. The four intended learning outcomes read like the four kinds of exam question you will meet: describe and explain the common beneficiation processes; analyse why a process is selected from the raw material's properties; generalise that knowledge to suggest a process for a hypothetical raw material; and calculate the technical-economic conditions of winning a mineral resource. The first two are the essay questions of the old papers, the third is the "what would you do with an ore that…" question, and the fourth is the balance arithmetic of topics T10 and T11 and the economics of period two.

2. Two periods, four examinations

Period one is the processing of metal ores: autogenous grinding, the theory of flotation, copper, lead, zinc and complex sulphide ores, magnetite and hematite ores, with three assignments (product balance; limiting grades and selectivity; mass and water balance) and a computer laboratory class on mass balancing. Period two is particle technology (bulk solids handling, fine grinding), industrial rocks and minerals and solid fuels (aggregates, cement, functional minerals, coal) and environmental aspects (soil washing, recycling of aggregates), with three more assignments (revenues, cost analysis, technical-economical calculations). The examination table has four rows: 0001, the written exam of period one, 2.4 credits on the scale U, 3, 4, 5; 0002, the oral exam of period two, 3 credits on the same scale; 0003, the laboratory class and assignments, 1.5 credits, pass or fail; 0004, the field trip, 0.6 credits, pass or fail. The grade limits used on the old papers are 50, 65 and 80 % of the points for grades 3, 4 and 5.

3. The people and the trips

The lecturers of the introduction are Saeed Chelgani (the examiner of the 2020 and 2022 papers), Sina Shakibania, Mehdi Parian (the circuit-balancing compendium and the lab) and Olof Martinsson (the two geology lectures); the head of subject is Jan Rosenkranz. The study trip goes with the Geometallurgy course to Lapland for the EMerald students, to Aitik and, for the process metallurgy group, to Garpenberg in Bergslagen: the two plants that the copper and the lead-zinc lessons keep citing, seen from the inside. Last year's course evaluation, shown in the lecture, rated the course 3.7 of 5 overall with the workload judged a little on the heavy side, which is a fair warning about the assignment weeks.

4. The toolbox: formulas and the dilution ratio

Two documents in the introduction folder are worth printing. The Compilation of Mineral Processing Formulas (Pålsson, version 1.2) is the course's formula sheet, and its sections are the skeleton of the calculation questions: product balances (mass and element balances, recovery, the two-product formula), water balances (dilution ratio, per cent solids, pulp density), circuit balancing (nodes, least squares), hydrocyclone and comminution relations. The written exams attach the balance pages of it as an appendix, so the point is not to memorise them but to know which one to open. The Slurry density and dilution ratio sheet derives the one relation every water question needs:

D=ρl(ρs−ρp)ρs(ρp−ρl),Fw=1001+D

which turns a Marcy-scale reading into the water per tonne of solids. The circuit-balancing lesson works through both.

5. How the written exam is built, from the six old papers

Every paper of 2014 to 2022 has the same shape: four hours, a calculator, seven or eight questions, "answers must be complete and show intermediate steps" on the cover, the formula appendix at the back. Five or six questions are essays worth about 6 points each on the metal-ore modules (one on flotation chemistry with a Bjerrum diagram, one on reagents, one on autogenous grinding, one or two on copper, lead-zinc, iron, gold or the alloying metals, sometimes one on mining methods), and two are calculations worth about 10 points each: a water balance with dilution ratios and a circulating load, and a products balance worksheet. Malla's exam bank holds all 46 questions with answers, sorted by paper and by topic, and each lesson ends with the ones that belong to its module. A six-point essay is three ideas with a reason each; a ten-point balance is the equations in symbols, the numbers with units, and a check that it closes.

6. Reading this course with Malla

The eleven modules of Mineral Processing in Malla follow the course's own material folders, and each module page has three layers: the lesson (this kind of reading, written from the slides and compendia with its sources listed), the slides themselves to open on the PC, and the exam questions of the module with their answers folded. The suggested order for period one is the order of the written exam's weight: circuit balancing (lab and two calculation questions), flotation theory, autogenous grinding, copper, lead-zinc, iron, then gold, the alloying metals and the two geology lectures as the reasons behind the rest. Two rules from the course's own page on assessment travel with everything: what you hand in must be your group's analysis, with any language help declared; and every number you present must be one you can defend at the seminar.

Worked example

Where the points are. Take the 2014 paper: 56 points in eight questions, of which questions 7 (water balance, 10 points) and 8 (products balance, 10 points) are 36 % of the total, and the six essays the other 64 %. Grade 3 needs 28 points, grade 4 needs 36.4, grade 5 needs 44.8. A student who does both balances fully (20 points) needs only 8 of the 36 essay points for a pass and 25 for a 5; a student who skips the balances must score 28 of 36 on essays for a pass. The balances are the most predictable questions on the paper, which is why Malla wrote them first, and why the circuit-balancing lesson comes first in the reading order.

How the exam asks it

The introduction is not examined, but it explains the examiner. The learning outcomes say the questions will ask you to describe, analyse, generalise and calculate, in that mix; the examination table says the written exam covers period one only; the cover page of every old paper says intermediate steps are marked. Read the six old papers once, with the exam bank open, before the first assignment: after that, every lesson reads as an answer key.
