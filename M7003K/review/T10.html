Water balance: dilution ratios and circulating load

6 questions across the papers · module 4

The pattern that solves all of them
  1. Write the solids balance first, at steady state: what goes in comes out. Around the whole circuit, feed solids = sum of the product solids, which fixes the unknown product.
  2. Around each unit, solids in = solids out; this gives every internal solids flow (cyclone feed = underflow + overflow, and so on).
  3. Water travels as dilution ratio D = water / solids, so water flow W = D x M. Fresh water added anywhere ends up in the products; write the water balance around the whole circuit to find the unknown product dilution.
  4. Then the water balance around each unit gives the remaining dilution ratios (mix: W_in = W_out).
  5. Circulating load = recirculating solids / fresh feed solids x 100 %. Check: every D must be positive and the numbers must close both balances (the 2014 official solution: 2.28, 0.89, 1.05 and 184 %).

The variants, year by year

Exam Sp 1, 2014-10-27 · Ex. 7 · 10 p · T10 · the pilot-circuit water balances · module 4

Variant A · 2014-10-27 · question 7

Water balance of a pilot circuit (mill, hydrocyclone, Wright Impact Tray): dilution ratios of the cyclone overflow, cyclone feed and WIT tailings, and the circulating load, from the solids flows, three water additions and two known dilution ratios.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

The examiner's solution to this question exists (2014 paper); it is on the PC copy.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

What the question really asks. Three streams leave or circulate without a dilution ratio (cyclone overflow, cyclone feed, WIT tailings). Everything else is given. Two balances solve it: solids first, then water. The examiner's own answer is in the fold above; the numbers below reproduce it.

Note

Words used here. Dilution ratio D=water/solids of a stream: kilograms of water per kilogram of solids (1 litre of water weighs 1 kg, so l/min of water divided by kg/min of solids). Balance: "what goes in equals what comes out" around a box you draw yourself, either the whole circuit or one unit. Circulating load: the solids that go round again, as a percentage of the fresh feed.

Step 1. Solids around the whole circuit. At steady state what goes in comes out. Only two streams leave: the cyclone overflow and the WIT concentrate.

overflow=feed−concentrate=14.3−3.2=11.1kg/min

Step 2. Solids around each unit.

  • Cyclone: feed=underflow+overflow=29.5+11.1=40.6kg/min.
  • WIT: it receives the cyclone underflow (29.5) and splits it into concentrate (3.2) and tailings: 29.5−3.2=26.3kg/min, which go back to the mill.

Step 3. Circulating load. The recirculating solids divided by the fresh feed:

CL=solids back to the millfresh feed×100=26.314.3×100=184%

Step 4. Water around the whole circuit. Dilution ratio D=water/solids, so water=D×solids (1 l of water is 1 kg). All the fresh water, 8.5+9.0+10.7=28.2 l/min, leaves with the two products:

Woverflow=28.2−3.2×0.92=28.2−2.94=25.26l/min⇒Doverflow=25.2611.1=2.28

Step 5. Water around the cyclone. Its feed water is the sum of the two outlets:

Wcyclone feed=29.5×0.37+25.26=10.92+25.26=36.17⇒Dcyclone feed=36.1740.6=0.89

Step 6. Water around the WIT. In: the underflow water plus the two water additions; out: concentrate and tailings.

Wtailings=10.92+9.0+10.7−2.94=27.67⇒Dtailings=27.6726.3=1.05

Step 7. Close the loop. Mill water in = 8.5 (fresh) +27.67 (tailings) =36.17 = the cyclone feed water found in step 5. The balance closes, so the three answers are consistent.

Feed ore14.300
Cyclone feed40.636.20.89

WIT concentrate (product)3.22.90.92

Watch out

The circulating load uses the fresh feed as the base (26.3/14.3), not the cyclone feed. And the deck-2 water enters the WIT, so it leaves through the tailings or the concentrate, never through the cyclone overflow directly. Both are the classic slips on this question.

Note

Marking in 2014: 6 points for the three dilution ratios, 4 for the circulating load. Write every balance as an equation before putting numbers in; the examiner asks for the intermediate steps.

Variant B · 2014-12-22 · question 7

Water balance of a pilot circuit where a hydrocyclone overflow is re-classified in a lamella thickener: dilution ratios of the cyclone overflow, cyclone underflow and thickener underflow, and the circulating load.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

Read the flowsheet before the numbers. Only one fresh-water addition exists (25.8 l/min at the mill), yet the lamella overflow carries 5.7×36.0=205 l/min of water. That is only possible if the lamella overflow goes back into the circuit (to the pump) and the lamella underflow is the product that leaves. With that reading every balance closes; with any other it does not. Say this in the exam: reading the diagram is part of the answer.

Note

Words used here. Dilution ratio D=water/solids of a stream (1 l of water is 1 kg). Lamella thickener: a tank with inclined plates where solids settle; its underflow is the thick pulp at the bottom, its overflow the almost clear water on top. Circulating load: solids that go round again, as a percentage of the fresh feed.

Step 1. Solids around the whole circuit. The only exit is the lamella underflow: 18.9 kg/min, equal to the feed.

Step 2. Solids around the lamella. Its feed is the cyclone overflow:

cyclone overflow=lamella underflow+lamella overflow=18.9+5.7=24.6kg/min

Step 3. Solids around the cyclone. underflow=49.7−24.6=25.1kg/min, back to the mill.

Step 4. Circulating load. Recycle to the mill divided by the fresh feed:

CL=25.118.9×100=133%

If the thickener overflow that returns to the pump is counted as recirculating solids as well, (25.1+5.7)/18.9=163%. Give the first number and say which streams you counted.

Step 5. Water around the whole circuit. In: 25.8 l/min. Out: only the lamella underflow.

Wlamella underflow=25.8l/min⇒D=25.818.9=1.37

Step 6. Water around the pump and cyclone. Cyclone feed water =49.7×4.88=242.5 l/min. It is the sum of what the pump receives: the mill discharge water (25.8 fresh plus the cyclone underflow water Wu, since the mill only gets those two) plus the thickener overflow water 5.7×36.0=205.2:

242.5=25.8+Wu+205.2⇒Wu=11.5l/min⇒Dcyclone underflow=11.525.1=0.46

Step 7. Cyclone overflow.

Wcyclone overflow=242.5−11.5=231.0⇒Dcyclone overflow=231.024.6=9.39

Step 8. Check at the lamella. Water in 231.0 = out 205.2+25.8=231.0. Closed.

StreamSolids kg/minWater l/minD
Feed ore18.900
Cyclone feed49.7242.54.88
Cyclone underflow (to mill)25.111.50.46
Cyclone overflow (to lamella)24.6231.09.39
Lamella overflow (to pump)5.7205.236.0
Lamella underflow (product)18.925.81.37
Why

A dilution ratio of 36 means 97 % water: the lamella overflow is the wash water of the circuit, which is why it is recycled to the pump and not thrown away. A dilution ratio of 0.46 in the cyclone underflow (68 % solids) is a normal dense underflow. The numbers tell you whether you read the diagram right.

Variant C · 2015-10-26 · question 7

Water balance of a pilot circuit where a hydrocyclone underflow is upgraded in a Hukki cone classifier: dilution ratios of the cyclone overflow and the cone-classifier overflow, and the circulating load.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

What changes against the WIT variants. The gravity unit is now a cone classifier (Hukki), and the exam gives the classifier underflow (23.8 kg/min, D 0.89) instead of a concentrate. Two product streams leave the circuit: the cyclone overflow and the classifier overflow. The classifier underflow, with a water addition, goes back to the mill.

Note

Words used here. Dilution ratio D=water/solids (1 l of water is 1 kg). Cone classifier: a conical tank; coarse or heavy grains settle to the underflow at the apex, fine ones leave with the overflow at the rim. Circulating load: the solids that return to the mill, as a percentage of the fresh feed.

Step 1. Solids around the classifier. Its feed is the cyclone underflow, 25.3 kg/min:

cone overflow=25.3−23.8=1.5kg/min

Step 2. Solids around the whole circuit. Feed 15.5 = cyclone overflow + cone overflow:

cyclone overflow=15.5−1.5=14.0kg/mincyclone feed=25.3+14.0=39.3kg/min

Step 3. Circulating load. The stream that returns to the mill is the cone underflow:

CL=23.815.5×100=154%

Step 4. Water around the classifier. In: underflow water 25.3×0.35=8.86 plus the 15.0 l/min added on the cyclone underflow, 23.86 in all. Out: cone underflow 23.8×0.89=21.18 plus the cone overflow water:

Wcone overflow=23.86−21.18=2.67⇒Dcone overflow=2.671.5=1.78

Step 5. Water around the whole circuit. Fresh water 17.9+15.0+10.7=43.6 l/min leaves with the two overflows:

Wcyclone overflow=43.6−2.67=40.93⇒Dcyclone overflow=40.9314.0=2.92

Step 6. Check at the mill. Water in =17.9+21.18 (cone underflow) +10.7 (added on it) =49.78. Cyclone feed water =8.86+40.93=49.78. Closed.

Feed ore15.500
Cyclone feed39.349.81.27
Cyclone underflow25.38.90.35
Cyclone overflow (product)14.040.92.92
Cone overflow (product)1.52.71.78

Watch out

The 10.7 l/min is added to the cone underflow on its way to the mill, after the classifier. It belongs to the mill's water balance, not to the classifier's. Put it in the wrong balance and the cone overflow comes out with a negative water flow, which is the signal to look again.

Retake Sp 1, 2015-12-22 · Ex. 7 · 10 p · T10 · the pilot-circuit water balances · module 4

Variant D · 2015-12-22 · question 7

Water balance of a three-mill grinding circuit (rod mill, two ball mills, two cyclones) from solids flows, moisture, dilution ratios and two known water additions: water added to each mill feed, dilution ratio of the flotation feed, and the solids flow to each cyclone.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

The hardest of the water balances, and the most like a real plant. Three mills, two cyclones, water added at the feed and at the discharge of the mills. The trick is to see that the table gives a dilution ratio for every pumped stream, so every mill can be balanced on its own once you know its solids.

Note

Words used here. Dilution ratio D=water/solids (1 m³ of water is 1 t). Closed circuit: a mill whose discharge goes to a cyclone, and whose cyclone underflow (the coarse part) comes back to the same mill. Moisture of the feed ore: water as a percentage of the wet mass. Circulating load: solids back to a mill, as a percentage of the fresh feed.

Reading the table. The row "Pump, 0.76" is the rod-mill discharge as it is pumped; "Feed cyclone 2, 0.84" is that pulp after the ball-mill-2 discharge has joined it. Only with this reading are there as many equations as unknowns; state it in your answer. Moisture 4.57 % is on the wet mass, so the water in the feed is

Wfeed=52.5×4.5795.43=2.51m3/h

(1 m³ of water is 1 t).

c) Solids to each cyclone (do this first, it is the backbone).

  • Cyclone 2 feed = rod-mill discharge 52.5 + ball-mill-2 discharge 31.2 (the cyclone 2 underflow returns through the mill): 52.5+31.2=83.7t/h. Its overflow =83.7−31.2=52.5 t/h, which is the fresh feed again: nothing accumulates.
  • Cyclone 3 feed = cyclone 2 overflow 52.5 + ball-mill-3 discharge 24.1: 52.5+24.1=76.6t/h. Its overflow =76.6−24.1=52.5 t/h to flotation.

a) Water added to each mill feed. Work along the pulp.

  1. Rod mill: discharge water =52.5×0.76=39.90 m³/h. In: 2.51 (moisture) +x1+12.9 (discharge water). So x1=39.90−2.51−12.9=24.5m3/h.
  2. Cyclone 2 feed water =83.7×0.84=70.31. The ball-mill-2 discharge brings 70.31−39.90=30.41. In to ball mill 2: cyclone 2 underflow water 31.2×0.38=11.86, plus x2, plus 5.9. So x2=30.41−11.86−5.9=12.7m3/h.
  3. Cyclone 2 overflow water =70.31−11.86=58.45 (with 52.5 t/h, D=1.11). Cyclone 3 feed water =76.6×1.04=79.66, so ball mill 3 discharges 79.66−58.45=21.21. In to ball mill 3: cyclone 3 underflow 24.1×0.57=13.74, plus x3. So x3=21.21−13.74=7.5m3/h.

b) Dilution ratio of the flotation feed. Cyclone 3 overflow water =79.66−13.74=65.93 m³/h with 52.5 t/h:

Dflotation feed=65.9352.5=1.26(44%solids)

Feed ore52.52.50.05
Rod mill discharge (pump)52.539.90.76
Cyclone 2 feed83.770.30.84
Cyclone 2 underflow31.211.90.38
Cyclone 2 overflow52.558.51.11
Cyclone 3 feed76.679.71.04

Check. Total water added =2.51+24.5+12.9+12.7+5.9+7.5=65.9 m³/h = the water leaving with the flotation feed. Closed.

Watch out

If you take "Pump 0.76" as the dilution of the combined pump feed, x1 and x2 can only be found as a sum (30.4 m³/h) and the question has no unique answer. When a data set only works one way, that way is the intended one; write the assumption down and move on.

Written exam, 2020-10-26 · Ex. 7 · 10 p · T10 · the pilot-circuit water balances · module 4

Variant E · 2020-10-26 · question 7

Water balance of the mill, hydrocyclone and Wright Impact Tray pilot circuit (variant with 15.8 kg/min feed and 10.0 l/min mill water): dilution ratios of the cyclone overflow, cyclone feed and WIT tailings, and the circulating load.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

Same circuit as the 2014 paper, two numbers changed: the feed is 15.8 kg/min instead of 14.3 and the mill water 10.0 l/min instead of 8.5. The cyclone underflow, the WIT concentrate and the other two water additions are identical. Read variant A (2014) for the drawing and the reasoning; here are the numbers.

Step 1. Solids around the whole circuit.

overflow=feed−concentrate=15.8−3.2=12.6kg/min

Step 2. Solids around each unit. Cyclone feed =29.5+12.6=42.1kg/min. WIT tailings =29.5−3.2=26.3kg/min (unchanged, because the underflow and the concentrate did not change).

Step 3. Circulating load.

CL=26.315.8×100=166%

(lower than 2014 because the same recycle is compared with a larger feed).

Step 4. Water around the whole circuit. Fresh water 10.0+9.0+10.7=29.7 l/min leaves with the two products:

Woverflow=29.7−3.2×0.92=29.7−2.94=26.76⇒Doverflow=26.7612.6=2.12

Step 5. Water around the cyclone.

Wcyclone feed=29.5×0.37+26.76=10.92+26.76=37.67⇒Dcyclone feed=37.6742.1=0.89

Step 6. Water around the WIT.

Wtailings=10.92+9.0+10.7−2.94=27.67⇒Dtailings=27.6726.3=1.05

(unchanged: nothing on the WIT side moved).

Step 7. Check. Mill water in =10.0+27.67=37.67 = cyclone feed water. Closed.
