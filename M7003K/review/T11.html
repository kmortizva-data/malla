M7003K · review · T11

Products balance and the two-product formula

6 questions across the papers · module 4 · module 6 · module 7

The pattern that solves all of them
  1. Draw the flowsheet with the streams named (feed, concentrates, middlings, final tail); the balance table has one row per stream.
  2. Concentrate mass from recovery: M_c = R x M_f x f / c (recovery fixes the mass when grade and recovery are given), or from grades alone with the two-product formula M_c = M_f (f - t)/(c - t).
  3. Fill the metal columns: metal = mass x grade; distribution (%) = metal in stream / metal in feed x 100. For several concentrates work stream by stream: feed minus concentrate 1 gives the middlings that feed stage 2.
  4. Concentrator difference = feed metal minus the sum of the products' metal (zero for the elements whose recovery was given, because the recovery defined the masses; non-zero for by-product elements such as silver). Concentrator loss = metal in the tail per tonne of feed (g/t or kg/t).
  5. Recovery R = c(f - t)/(f(c - t)) x 100 when masses are unknown; always check that masses and metal close to the feed.

Variant A · 2014-10-27 · question 8

Monthly products balance of a lead-zinc-silver concentrator (selective flotation, lead and zinc cakes with given grades and recoveries): flowsheet, full balance on the worksheet, concentrator differences and losses for lead, zinc and silver, and the silver recovery.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

The examiner's solution to this question exists (2014 paper); it is on the PC copy.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

The examiner's own table is in the fold above. Here is how to build it in the order that never gets stuck, with the numbers rounded the way the worksheet wants them.

Note

Words used here. Grade: the content of a metal in a stream (% or g/t). Recovery R: the fraction of a metal in the feed that ends up in its own concentrate. Cake: the filtered concentrate. Concentrator difference: metal in the feed minus metal found in all products; zero when the balance closes by construction, a real number when only assays are given. Concentrator loss: metal that did not reach its own concentrate, per tonne of feed.

Step 1. Metal in the feed. Mass times grade:

110000t×1.50%=1650tPb,110000×8.00%=8800tZn,110000×30g/t=3300kgAg

Step 2. Concentrate masses from the recoveries. A recovery fixes the metal in the concentrate, the grade then fixes the mass:

mPb cake=R×Pb in feedgrade=0.905×16500.700=1493.250.700=2133.2t
mZn cake=0.96×88000.550=84480.550=15360.0t

Step 3. The intermediate stream and the tail by difference.

  • mpPb (what leaves the lead circuit) =110000−2133.2=107866.8 t.
  • Final tail =107866.8−15360.0=92506.8t.

Step 4. Fill the metal columns. The final tail has given grades: 92506.8×0.10%=92.51 t Pb, ×0.30%=277.52 t Zn, ×5g/t=462.5 kg Ag. The grades that are not given come out by difference, so that the elements whose recoveries were used close exactly:

  • Zn in the Pb cake =8800−8448−277.52=74.48 t ⇒74.48/2133.2=3.49% Zn in the lead cake.
  • Pb in the Zn cake =1650−1493.25−92.51=64.24 t ⇒64.24/15360=0.42% Pb in the zinc cake.

Step 5. Silver, the by-product. Its grades are given everywhere, so it does NOT close: Pb cake 2133.2×890=1898.6 kg; Zn cake 15360×50=768.0 kg; tail 462.5 kg; sum 3 129.1 kg against 3 300 kg in the feed.

  • Concentrator difference (Ag) =3300−3129.1=−170.9kg (2 g/t of feed): the assays do not account for 5 % of the silver.
  • Concentrator recovery (CRY) of Ag =(1898.6+768.0)/3300=80.8% (60.7 % in the lead cake, 24.5 % in the zinc cake; the official table's distribution column gives 60.7 + 24.5, or 85.2 % if the tail is taken as the only loss).
  • Concentrator loss (CLS) of Ag =462.5kg/110000t=4.2g/t; the official table reports 11.19 g/t when the concentrator difference is counted as lost silver too ((462.5+170.9+…)/110000): state which definition you use.

Step 6. Lead and zinc closing numbers. Concentrator difference =0 for both (by construction, the recoveries define the masses). Concentrator loss = tail metal per tonne of feed: Pb 92.51t/110000t=0.84kg/t; Zn 277.52/110000=2.52kg/t. The official worksheet lists 1.43 and 3.20 kg/t, which are the losses per tonne of feed including the metal misplaced in the other concentrate: Pb 92.51+64.24=156.75 t ⇒1.43 kg/t; Zn 277.52+74.48=352.0 t ⇒3.20 kg/t. Use the examiner's definition in the exam: loss = everything that did not reach its own concentrate.

Variant B · 2014-12-22 · question 8

Daily products balance of the Aitik copper concentrator (rougher-scavenger and cleaner circuit; gold, copper and sulphur grades of feed, rougher concentrate, scavenger tail, final concentrate and cleaner tail): full balance, concentrator differences, recoveries and losses.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

What is different here: no recoveries are given, only grades, and there are three elements (gold, copper, sulphur). The masses come from the two-product formula applied twice, on copper, the element with the biggest contrast between streams. Gold and sulphur then ride along on the masses, and their concentrator differences are real numbers, not zero.

Note

Words used here. Two-product formula: when a feed F of grade f splits into a concentrate of grade c and a tail of grade t, a metal balance gives the concentrate mass without weighing anything: C=Ff−tc−t. Concentrator difference: metal in the feed minus metal in all products. CRY: concentrator recovery, metal in the final concentrate over metal in the feed. CLS: concentrator loss, metal in the tails per tonne of feed.

Step 1. Rougher-scavenger section, two-product formula on copper. The section takes the feed (0.28 % Cu) and produces the rougher concentrate (12.0 %) and the scavenger tail (0.022 %):

Mrougher conc=Ff−tc−t=92500×0.28−0.02212.0−0.022=92500×0.25811.978=1992.4t

Scavenger tail =92500−1992.4=90507.6t.

Step 2. Cleaner section, the same formula again. Its feed is the rougher concentrate (12.0 %); products: final concentrate (29.0 %) and cleaner tail (0.39 %):

Mfinal conc=1992.4×12.0−0.3929.0−0.39=1992.4×11.6128.61=808.5t

Cleaner tail =1992.4−808.5=1183.9t.

Step 3. Metal columns from the grades. Multiply every mass by every grade (gold in g → kg, copper and sulphur in t).

b) Differences, recoveries and losses.

  • Copper: difference 0 (its grades sized the streams). CRY=234.47/259.00=90.5%. CLS=(4.62+19.91)/92500=0.265kg/t.
  • Gold: difference =27.75−19.33=8.42kg, i.e. 30 % of the feed gold is not found in the products: the gold assays (0.3 g/t in the feed, close to detection limits) do not hang together with the copper-based masses. CRY=7.44/27.75=26.8% (or 38.5 % of the gold actually accounted for). CLS=(2.84+9.05)kg/92500t=0.129g/t.
  • Sulphur: difference =1110.0−1078.2=31.8t (2.9 %), an ordinary assay mismatch. CRY=250.6/1110.0=22.6% (most pyrite is meant to go to the tail). CLS=(13.0+814.6)/92500=8.95kg/t.
Why

Why the gold difference is so large: the two-product formula trusts the copper assays completely. Gold at 0.3 g/t is analysed with an error of the same order as the value, so 30 % "missing" gold is an assay problem, not a plant problem. In the exam, say exactly that: the concentrator difference is a diagnostic of the sampling and assays, and it is largest for the element with the worst analytical precision.

Note

The scavenger concentrate is an internal stream; it returns to the rougher feed and does not appear on the worksheet. Only streams that cross the plant boundary do.

What changed against 2014: collective flotation first (a bulk Pb-Zn concentrate), then a separation step; no silver; and this time the tail grades are given for both metals. The arithmetic is the same: recoveries fix the concentrate masses, everything else follows by difference.

Words used here. Collective (bulk) flotation: lead and zinc minerals floated together into one concentrate, which is then split in a separation stage. Variant D · 2015-12-22 · question 8

Column-flotation trial results by sieve fraction (concentrate and tailings weights and lead and zinc grades): recoveries by fraction and in total for lead and zinc, and the average grades of concentrate and tailings.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

A products balance by size fraction. The trial gives the total mass rates (1.02 kg/h of concentrate, 27.8 kg/h of tailings) and, for each sieve fraction, the sample weight and the grades. The recovery of a fraction is the metal in the concentrate of that fraction divided by the metal in concentrate plus tailings of the same fraction. No feed sample is needed: feed = concentrate + tailings.

Note

Words used here. Size fraction: the material caught between two sieves (150 µm, 106 µm, ...). Sample weight: the grams of each fraction in the laboratory sieving; it only tells you the proportions of the stream, not its rate. Recovery by fraction: how much of the metal of that size ended in the concentrate.

Step 1. Turn sample weights into mass rates. The sample weights only give the size distribution. Concentrate sample total = 28.87 g, tailings sample total = 193.69 g. Mass rate of a fraction:

m˙fraction=m˙stream×wfractionwsample

Step 2. Metal per fraction = mass rate × grade. Then

R=metal in concmetal in conc+metal in tail×100

Why

Why a size-by-size balance is asked: it shows where a flotation cell loses metal. Here the losses sit at the coarse end (locked or too heavy to lift, 0 % at 150 µm) and in the slimes (< 38 µm, 82 g/h of lead in the tailings). A plant metallurgist reads that as "grind a little finer, but not too fine, and consider a slimes stage".

Watch out

Do not weight the grades by the sample weights (grams): the sample weights are only proportions. Always convert to kg/h with the total rates first; the two samples were taken from streams of very different size, 1.02 against 27.8 kg/h.

Variant E · 2020-10-26 · question 6

Flotation circuit of a simple copper ore with rougher, scavenger, cleaner and recleaner: mass flows and grades at the unknown points from the known ones, and the total copper recovery.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

The method: a node balance at every junction. Around each unit or mixing point, solids in = solids out and copper in = copper out (copper = mass × grade). Start at the points where two of the three numbers are known and work around the circuit; the unknown points fall out one by one.

Note

Words used here. Node: any place where streams meet or split (a cell, a mixing point). Copper units: tonnes per hour multiplied by the % Cu; dividing units by a mass gives the grade back. Rougher, scavenger, cleaner, recleaner: the first cell, the cell that re-treats its tail, the cell that upgrades its concentrate, and the cell that upgrades that concentrate again. Recovery R: copper in the final concentrate as a fraction of copper in the feed.

Copper units. Multiply t/h by % Cu and call the product "copper units" (t/h×%); dividing by a mass gives the grade back.

Step 1. Mixing point in front of the rougher. In: fresh feed 100 t/h plus the two recycles; out: 112.42 t/h at 0.60 %. So the recycles carry 112.42−100=12.42 t/h and

112.42×0.60−100×0.54=67.45−54.00=13.45copper units

Step 2. Scavenger. Feed 105.66×0.12=12.68 units. Tail 1 takes 94.94×0.039=3.70 units.

scavenger conc=105.66−94.94=10.72t/h,12.68−3.70=8.98units⇒8.9810.72=0.84%Cu

Step 3. The other recycle (from the cleaner-scavenger).

12.42−10.72=1.70t/h,13.45−8.98=4.47units⇒4.471.70=2.63%Cu

Step 4. Rougher.

rougher conc=112.42−105.66=6.76t/h,67.45−12.68=54.77units⇒54.776.76=8.10%Cu

Step 5. Cleaner feed. Rougher concentrate plus the recleaner tail:
