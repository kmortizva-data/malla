Circuit balancing: the theory behind the lab

1 question across the papers · module 4

The pattern that solves all of them
  1. A node is a point in the flowsheet where streams meet or split; balancing writes conservation of mass and of each component around every node.
  2. A normal node has one product split into two (or two feeds into one). Grinding circuits contain non-normal nodes (a mill with feed and discharge but no separation), which carry no compositional information.
  3. Good balancing data have contrast: components whose grades differ strongly between the streams pin down the split; noisy assays (gold) are given large standard deviations.
  4. Software such as MATBAL takes assays with their relative standard deviations; weight-% solids must be converted to a component (water or solids flow) before it can be used.
  5. A relative standard deviation above 1 means the measurement is worse than useless: the adjustment then moves the value freely.

The variants, year by year

Retake Sp 1, 2014-12-22 · Ex. 5 · 6 p · T9 · nodes, contrast, MATBAL, standard deviations · module 4

Variant A · 2014-12-22 · question 5

Six true-or-false statements about circuit balancing: nodes, non-normal nodes in grinding circuits, gold assays, contrast, weight-% solids in MATBAL and relative standard deviations.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

Six statements, one point each, and the point is only safe when you can say why. Answers in the course's own terms (the circuit-balancing lab and MATBAL).

StatementAnswerWhy
aA node is a connection point in a flowsheet.TrueA node is any point where streams meet or split: a separator (one in, two out), a mixer (two in, one out) or a machine. Balances are written around nodes.
bA non-normal node can be found in a grinding circuit.TrueA normal node splits one stream into two or merges two into one and carries compositional information. A mill has one feed and one discharge with the same composition (no separation), and a cyclone-plus-mill loop can be reduced to nodes with more than two products: both are non-normal and typical of grinding circuits.
cAnalyses of gold are uncertain.TrueGold grades are low (grams per tonne) and the grains are coarse and rare, so sampling and assay errors are large. In a balance, gold gets a large standard deviation or is left out of the fit.
dThe best is to use analyses with large contrast when balancing over a process section.TrueAn element whose grade differs strongly between the products (e.g. copper between concentrate and tail) pins the split; an element with the same grade everywhere says nothing about the mass balance.
eInformation about weight % solids can be used directly in MATBAL.FalsePercent solids is not a component that is conserved on its own; it must be turned into a water flow or a dilution ratio (water treated as a component, or the solids and pulp masses given separately) before the program can use it.
fA relative standard deviation > 1.0 for elements or streams is good.FalseA relative standard deviation above 1 means the error is larger than the value itself: the measurement carries no information and the adjustment will move it anywhere. Good data have relative standard deviations of a few percent.
Why

The pattern behind the six: a balance needs conserved quantities (masses, metal masses, water), measured with known precision, on streams that a separation actually changes. Anything that fails one of the three (percent solids is not conserved, gold is imprecise, a mill does not separate) has to be converted, weighted down or dropped. The lab material CircuitBalancing_MP.pdf and Lab_CircuitBalancing_MP.pdf in module 4 are the source.
