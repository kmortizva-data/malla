M7003K · review · T8

Mining methods

2 questions across the papers · module 3

The pattern that solves all of them
  1. Say what kind of ore body the method suits (dip, size, continuity, rock strength).
  2. List the operations in order: development, drifting, drilling, charging and blasting, mucking, transport and hoisting.
  3. Give the prerequisites: sub-level caving needs a stable footwall and an ore and hanging wall that cave; cut-and-fill needs a fill material and ground that stands while the slice is mined.
  4. For cut-and-fill name the fills (waste rock, hydraulic sand fill, cemented or paste fill from tailings), how they are produced and what is demanded of them (strength, drainage, low cement cost).
  5. Connect to processing: how the method affects dilution, ore grade and the size of the run-of-mine feed.

The variants, year by year

Exam Sp 1, 2014-10-27 · Ex. 6 · 6 p · T8 · sub-level caving, cut-and-fill · module 3

Variant A · 2014-10-27 · question 6

Asks for the main operations of a sub-level caving mine and the prerequisites of the method.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

The examiner's solution to this question exists (2014 paper); it is on the PC copy.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

The examiner's answer is in the fold above: prerequisites (2 p) and the six operations (4 p). Here is the mental picture that makes the list natural.

The idea of sub-level caving. The mine is developed from the footwall side in horizontal levels 20 to 30 m apart (the sub-levels). From each sub-level, drifts are driven across the ore body. Above each drift the ore is drilled in fans and blasted slice by slice, from the hanging wall towards the footwall; as the blasted ore is drawn out at the drift, the rock above caves and follows it down. Nothing is left standing: the hanging wall is allowed to collapse into the void. LKAB's Kiruna mine is the world's largest example.

Prerequisites.

  • A large, steeply dipping ore body with continuity at depth, so that the levels can be repeated downward for decades.
  • A stable footwall (competent gangue) to hold the permanent openings: ramp, shafts, footwall drifts, ore passes.
  • An ore and hanging wall that cave and break up when undercut, and surface land that may subside (the caving reaches the surface).
  • Tolerance for dilution and ore loss at the draw points, since waste from the cave mixes with the ore (the reason gravity or magnetic cobbing precedes the mill).

The operations, in order.

  1. Development: ramp or incline and the footwall drifts of each transport level; shafts and ore passes.
  2. Drifting: on every sub-level, drifts driven from the footwall into and across the ore.
  3. Production drilling: long-hole rigs drill fans of holes upward into the ore above each drift.
  4. Charging and blasting: one fan at a time; the blasted ore fills the draw point at the drift face.
  5. Mucking (loading): LHD machines load the broken ore from the draw point and tip it into ore passes in the footwall; drawing stops when the waste content becomes too high.
  6. Transport and hoisting: the ore passes feed the main level, where trains or trucks take the ore to crushers and shafts, and it is hoisted to the surface.
Why

For the mineral-processing course the point is the interface: sub-level caving delivers a run-of-mine feed with variable waste dilution and lumps up to a metre, which is why Kiruna has underground crushing, cobbing and autogenous grinding. Module 3 and 9 (the geology lectures with the mining methods) are the source, together with the 2014 solution.

Exam Sp 1, 2015-10-26 · Ex. 6 · 6 p · T8 · sub-level caving, cut-and-fill · module 3

Variant B · 2015-10-26 · question 6

Asks which fill materials are common in cut-and-fill mining, how they are produced and what is demanded of the fill.

The paper's own wording is on the PC copy of Malla; the exam papers belong to LTU.

Answer, step by step (Malla)

Three parts again: which fills, how they are made, what they must do. Two points each.

What cut-and-fill is, in one sentence. The ore is mined in horizontal slices from the bottom up (or top down); after each slice the void is filled, and the fill becomes the floor (or the roof) for the next slice, so the walls of narrow, irregular or weak ore bodies stay supported. Common in small and medium Swedish mines (Boliden area, Garpenberg, Zinkgruvan).

Types of fill in use today, and how they are won.

  1. Waste rock fill. Development waste (drifts, ramps) and sometimes crushed rock from surface quarries or pre-concentration rejects, tipped dry into the stope by LHD or truck. Cheapest where waste is available underground.
  2. Hydraulic fill (sand fill). Deslimed flotation tailings from the concentrator: the tailings are cycloned to remove the fines (slimes would not drain), the sand fraction is pumped as a slurry through boreholes and pipes into the stope, where the water drains off through barricades and is pumped out. Sometimes natural sand from eskers or gravel pits is used.
  3. Cemented fill, paste fill. Tailings (including the fine fraction) thickened to a paste, mixed with 3 to 7 % cement or slag binder, and pumped into the stope; it hardens, needs no drainage and can stand as a free wall when the neighbouring stope is mined. Cemented rock fill mixes waste rock with a cement slurry for the same reason.

Demands on the fill.

  • Strength and stiffness: it must carry LHD traffic and workers (floor) and stand as a vertical wall when the adjacent slice or stope is mined (cemented fills), without liquefying.
  • Drainage and placement: hydraulic fill must drain fast (hence the desliming and a permeability specification), the barricades must hold, and paste must be pumpable through long pipelines without segregating.
  • Small settlement and shrinkage, so the next slice starts on a firm, level floor.
  • Cost and availability: binder content is the main cost, so the grading of the tailings is chosen to need as little cement as possible; the fill must be available at the mine's own rate of production.
  • Environment and safety: using tailings as fill reduces the surface tailings pond; the fill must not release acid or metals, and the cement must not create dust or heat problems.
Why

The processing connection the examiner is after: the concentrator's tailings are the mine's fill, so the tailings' size distribution, water and chemistry are designed with the mine in mind (desliming cyclones, paste plants). Module 3 and 9 (mining methods with the geology) are the source.
