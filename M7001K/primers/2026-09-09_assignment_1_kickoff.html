What this session is

Wednesday 9 September, 14:45 to 16:15, computer lab F441a. The study plan calls week 37 Project work: Assignment 1 is released this week and defended at the seminar on Friday 18 September (13:00 and 14:45, F433, derived from the study plan and TimeEdit, not yet confirmed by the teacher). This slot is where the group opens the assignment sheet together and divides the work.

The sheet is Assignment_1.pdf (Bertil Pålsson, 2013), two pages, on the module page under Material. Its three parts: build your own circuit, simulate with simple models, simulate with advanced models.

Three ideas to arrive with

  1. The circuit is given, the machines are yours. The reference is a USIM PAC example (case5), an aggregate plant with two impact crushers and a VSI plus screens, making 0-40, 0-20 and 40-70 mm products with 10-20, 4-10 and a 0-4 rest fraction. The sheet then simplifies: screens 1 and 2 become one double-deck screen, streams 10 and 21 disappear, stream 31 is the 0-4 mm product directly, the impact crushers and the VSI are replaced by crushers you choose, and the two splitters are adjustable with 50 % as default. Everything else you may assume, as long as you say so in the report: assumptions are the first rubric criterion.
  1. The feed tells you how much crushing there is to do. 300 t/h, 2 % moisture, top size 600 mm, RRS with d′ = 150 mm and n = 1.26. By hand: P(d) = 1 − exp(−(d/150)^{1.26}) gives 1.0 % finer than 4 mm, 3.2 % finer than 10 mm, 7.6 % finer than 20 mm, 17 % finer than 40 mm, 32 % finer than 70 mm, 63.2 % at 150 mm, 91 % at 300 mm, 99.7 % at 600 mm. So roughly 250 of the 300 t/h are coarser than 40 mm and must go through at least one crusher; the products you sell are almost entirely made, not screened out.
  1. Simple first, advanced second, and the comparison is the point. Part 2 uses the ideal screen models (SCRN, DSC1: mesh size plus a transmission efficiency) and the simple crushers (JAW1, GYRA, CRS1: a setting and a standard product curve) to get a circuit that makes the fractions without overloading anything. Part 3 saves the circuit under a new name, switches the screens to Karra (SCR2, DSC2) with no deck larger than 3 × 6 m, replaces the crushers with the cycle models (CRSH, SHHD) and picks real machine sizes from King's Tables 5.4 to 5.7, checking that each machine takes its feed within the manufacturer's capacity and power at that setting. The report then compares the two rounds: what changed and why.

Two questions to keep in mind

  • Which crusher goes where? The first machine sees 600 mm lumps at 300 t/h: a jaw or a gyratory with an OSS that Table 5.4 or 5.5 supports. The machines making 4-10 and 0-4 mm see small feed at lower tonnage: cone territory, Table 5.6 (standard) or 5.7 (short-head). The choice is defended at the seminar, so write the reason next to each machine.
  • What does "condensed machine data" mean for the report? For each unit: model, setting, size, tonnage in, power, area utilisation for screens. One table, not a paragraph. Decide now who fills it and from which MODSIM report.

Five terms

TermIn one line
RRS distribution (d′, n)Rosin-Rammler-Sperling: P(d) = 1 − exp(−(d/d′)^{n}); d′ is the size with 63.2 % finer, n the slope
Transmission efficiencyThe SCRN and DSC1 parameter: the share of undersize that actually passes the deck (90 % is the default)
Karra model (SCR2, DSC2)Screen model that computes the separation from deck size, aperture, wire, angle and load; it needs real deck dimensions, hence the 3 × 6 m limit
OSS / CSSOpen- and closed-side setting: JAW1, JAW2 and GYRA take the OSS; CRS1, CRSH (for cones) and SHHD take the CSS
Condensed machine dataThe per-unit table the report must carry: model, setting, size, feed tonnage, power, utilisation

Where it lands in the assignment

The rubric has five criteria worth 5 points each: problem definition and assumptions; flowsheet and material definition; model selection and parameters; simulation results; discussion and analysis. Part 2 covers the first four at grade-3 level; Part 3 and the comparison are where grades 4 and 5 live (interpret the results in process terms, motivate changes). The group presents at the seminar and opposes another group's work, so two people should be able to defend every machine choice.

Group habits that save the week: one shared folder; a PAK saved after every milestone with a name that says what it is (A1_simple_v1.PAK, A1_karra_v1.PAK); one person owns the flowsheet, one the machine tables from King, one the report and plots; and the course's AI rule in mind from day one: language help is fine, analysis and conclusions are yours, and any use is declared in the report.

Bring: the size classes and the RRS feed already entered (RRS.xls does the points), Tables5.4-5.7.pdf open, and the Exercise 1 station for the screening part, because a double-deck screen at 40 and 20 mm is the Exercise 1 problem with different numbers.
