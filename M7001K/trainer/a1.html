Assignment 1 trainer

Go to the window, choose the value, and find out whether it was the one the group used and why. Seven modules take you from the sheet to the seminar, and at the end you get the story of how twenty-four runs became one design. Before you start, keep in mind that MODSIM prints every size about 19 % too coarse. The run readings quote the sizes as MODSIM printed them, while the stations that check a machine against King's tables use the corrected sizes.

There are 80 stations, grouped in 7 modules. Each station is one decision you make in the assignment. It tells you where you are in MODSIM, asks what you would choose, and then explains why. If your answer is wrong, it shows the value the group used and the reason behind it. Your progress is saved in this browser.

What the colours mean
  • Circulating load. This is how much material the recycle loop sends back to the secondary crusher, compared with the fresh feed that enters the loop. The cell is green when the loop sends back less than it receives, which means 100 % or less. The line sits at 100 % because the runs fall into two clear groups. When the loop is stable, it sends back between 15 and 52 %, and when it gets out of control, it sends back between 178 and 523 %.
  • Screen-14. Screen-14 is the screen that closes the recycle loop, so when it is too small the whole loop overloads. The cell is green when two things are true. First, its area utilisation factor, which says how full the screen deck is, must be 1 or less, because above 1 the model itself reports the screen as overloaded. Second, its efficiency must be at least 60 %. The 60 % sits in the gap between the runs: with a stable loop the efficiency was between 69 and 98 %, and without one it fell to between 21 and 47 %.
  • Secondary cone. The group chose a 7 ft fine standard cone from King's Table 5.6 as the secondary crusher, and the cell is green only when this cone can really do the job in that run. That means four checks. The table must list a capacity at the run's closed-side setting, or CSS. The cone's feed opening, measured on the closed side, must be at least as wide as the opening the model asks for. Its capacity must cover the tonnage, and its motor must be at least as strong as the power the model calculates. If any check fails, the cell names the smallest cone in the table that would pass, or says that no cone in the table does. The opening and the power come from the model, corrected for the way MODSIM reads sizes. MODSIM reads every size about 19 % too coarse, so the opening it asks for is divided by 1.19, and its power estimate is multiplied by 1.09. The report and the A1 guide explain where the error comes from.
  • Primary jaw. The group chose a 36 × 48 jaw crusher from King's Table 5.4 as the primary crusher. The cell is green when three checks pass. First, the capacity in the column closest to the run's gap must cover the tonnage. Second, the largest rock the jaw can take, which is about 0.8 times its gape, must be at least as big as the top size of the feed. Third, its motor must cover the power the model calculates, corrected in the same way as for the secondary cone. When the jaw fails any of these checks, the cell also quotes the 30 × 60 gyratory crusher from Table 5.5, which is the alternative the group looked at.
  • Tertiary short head. The group chose a 3 ft extra coarse short-head cone from King's Table 5.7 as the tertiary crusher, and the cell is green when four checks pass. Its feed opening on the open side, which is 76 mm, must take the largest pieces of the feed. The run's CSS must not be below the machine's minimum setting of 5 mm. Its capacity must cover the tonnage, and its motor must cover the power the model calculates. The opening and the power are corrected in the same way as for the secondary cone. When the run's CSS falls between two settings listed in the table, the capacity is read on a straight line between them.
  • 0/20 product. The group defined the 0/20 product as gravel in which at least 90 % of the material is finer than 20 mm, so the cell is green from 90 % upwards. The runs show why this matters. When half of the 20 to 40 mm fraction goes into this product, only 72 % of it is finer than 20 mm, but when none of that fraction goes there, the figure is between 95 and 100 %.
  • 40/70 product. This cell turns red when the 40/70 stream is empty. A variation that makes one of the six products disappear no longer builds the plant that the sheet describes, so it cannot be compared fairly with the other runs.
  • Reduction ratios. The reduction ratio is the size of the feed divided by the size of the product, both taken as the d80, the size that 80 % of the material passes. The cell is green when the ratio falls inside King's rules of thumb, which are 4 to 9 for a jaw crusher, 6 to 8 for a secondary cone and 4 to 6 for a tertiary short head. A red ratio does not mean that the design fails, because the sheet only asks that no machine is overloaded. It is simply something the report should explain.
  • Verdict. A green verdict means that the change is part of the final design. A red verdict means that it was rejected. A verdict without colour marks an alternative that the report describes but does not choose.
