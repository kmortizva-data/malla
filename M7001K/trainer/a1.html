The rules behind the colours
  • Circulating load. Green at 100 % or less: the loop sends back less than the fresh feed it receives. The runs split there: 15 to 52 % when the loop settles, 178 to 523 % when it snowballs.
  • Screen-14. Green when the area utilisation factor is 1 or less, the model's own overload line, and the efficiency is at least 60 %. The 60 % sits in the gap of the runs: 69 to 98 % with a settled loop, 21 to 47 % without.
  • Secondary cone. Green when the group's 7 ft fine lists a capacity at the run's CSS in Table 5.6, its closed-side opening is at least the opening the model asks for, it covers the tonnage and its motor covers the model's power. Otherwise the cell names the smallest row that would, or says that none does.
  • Primary jaw. Green when the 36 × 48 jaw of Table 5.4 covers the tonnage in the column nearest the gap, 0.8 × gape is at least the top size and the motor covers the model's power. When it does not, the 30 × 60 gyratory of Table 5.5 is quoted.
  • Tertiary short head. Green when the 3 ft extra coarse short head of Table 5.7 takes the feed on its open side (76 mm), allows the CSS (minimum 5 mm), covers the tonnage and its motor covers the model's power. Between two listed settings the capacity is read on a straight line.
  • 0/20 product. Green when at least 90 % is below 20 mm, the group's definition of the product. The runs give 72 % with half the 20-40 mm inside and 95 to 100 % with none.
  • 40/70 product. Red when the stream carries nothing: a variation that removes a product is a different plant.
  • Reduction ratios. Green inside King's rules of thumb: jaw 4 to 9, secondary cone 6 to 8, tertiary short head 4 to 6. A red ratio is a remark to explain, not a failure of the sheet's criterion, which is no overload.
  • Verdict. Green: kept in the final design or recommended. Red: rejected. No colour: an alternative the report documents.
