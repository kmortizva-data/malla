Your first simulation: King's example 10.1 in MODSIM

module 1 · King 10.1-10.3

The exercise sheet and the worked files live on the PC copy of Malla; the sheet's numbers are transcribed in this guide.

What they ask

Canvas page 1.4 has two parts. Part A is drawing practice: place unit icons on a flowsheet, connect them with streams, insert a mixer, resize and rotate icons, delete and redraw. Part B is the first real run: unpack Ex10-1Screen.PAK (King's worked example 10.1, a screening problem), run it, look at every stream, and export the flowsheet and the particle size distributions the way the course wants them in reports. Nothing is graded; everything later assumes you did it.

Why (the engineering)

The exercise is small on purpose. A simulator is three descriptions glued together (material, machines, flowsheet), and the fastest way to see how MODSIM stores each of them is to open a job somebody else built and click through it: where the size classes live, where the feed is defined, what a unit report contains, and how a stream carries solids, water and a size distribution at once. The exporting part matters because every assignment presentation needs a flowsheet with fly-outs and a size-distribution plot, and the PostScript-to-EMF route is where people lose an evening the night before a seminar.

Before MODSIM: numbers by hand

Read King section 10.1 first and write down, from the example text, the feed tonnage, the aperture and the number of size classes. When the run finishes, the fly-out of the feed stream must show that tonnage to the decimal and the products must add up to it. If they do not, either the PAK did not unpack cleanly or you are looking at the wrong stream. Two numbers, checked before anything else, save the whole afternoon.

Step by step in MODSIM

1

Set the work area and the decimal point once

ClickWindows regional settings > decimal symbol "." ; environment variable MODSIM=C:\Temp\ (classic MODSIM only)
ExpectMODSIM reads and writes in C:\Temp\ instead of its own Program Files folder
WhyPage 1.3's three golden rules; the decimal comma is behind most "mysterious" errors
On the terminal server the variable is MODSIM=H:\MODSIM\
2

Part A, draw for ten minutes without caring about correctness

ClickGraphics editor: place a feed, a screen and two products; draw the streams; insert a mixer; select an icon and resize or rotate it; delete one stream and redraw it
ExpectStreams snap to the unit ports; a stream drawn to the wrong port can be deleted and redrawn
WhyIn classic MODSIM the meaning of a stream comes from WHERE it touches the icon (oversize port versus undersize port). Learning the ports now is what makes Exercise 1 quick
3

Part B, unpack the worked example

ClickClassic: File > Unpack a job > Ex10-1Screen.PAK (module 1 material, or MODSIM's own Jobs folder). GUI: File > Open > the same PAK
ExpectA flowsheet with a feed, a screen and two products appears with all its data already entered
Unpack into your work area, not into the program folder; then Save as a new job name
4

Run and read every stream

ClickRun the simulation. Click each stream to open its fly-out; open the screen's unit report
ExpectSolids flow, water flow and percent solids per stream; the size distribution of each stream; in the report, the screen's separation and efficiency
WhyThis is the material description, the flowsheet and the unit model seen from the result side
5

Export the flowsheet and a size-distribution plot the report way

ClickClassic: flowsheet view > export PostScript > convert the .ps to EMF with a free online converter > insert the EMF in Word. Repeat for the size distribution graph. GUI: export the graph directly
ExpectA vector figure that stays sharp at any zoom in the document
WhyPresentations and the final report need flowsheets with fly-outs and size plots; EMF is what keeps them readable
If a GUI plot differs from classic MODSIM, the course asks you to report it to the teacher with a screenshot and the PAK
6

Pack the job and keep it

ClickPack to a new .PAK in Simulation of Mineral Processing/Projects MODSIM
ExpectOne file that reopens on any machine with MODSIM
WhyThe PAK is the unit of saving, sharing and submitting in this course

Expected results

The products of the screen sum to the feed tonnage; the oversize is coarser than the aperture except for the near-size leakage a real screen model produces; the size-distribution plot shows three curves (feed, oversize, undersize) with the undersize entirely finer than the aperture region. The worked solution SolEx_B.doc in the module 1 material shows what Part B should look like: open it after your own run.

Common mistakes

Watch out

Working inside the program folder. Classic MODSIM throws its swarm of job files wherever the work area points; without the MODSIM variable that is Program Files, where it gets lost or write-protected.

Also: a decimal comma; unpacking two jobs into the same work folder so that files overwrite each other; forgetting that closing without packing leaves nothing portable; PNG screenshots of the flowsheet in the report instead of vector EMF.

Sensitivity analysis

None is asked. If you want one: change the screen aperture in the unpacked job by 10 % up and down and watch how the two products move; it is a two-minute preview of Exercise 1.
