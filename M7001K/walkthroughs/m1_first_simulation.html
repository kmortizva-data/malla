Your first simulation: King's example 10.1 in MODSIM

module 1 · King 10.1-10.3

Read in King: King 10.1–10.3 · The nature of simulation · p. 339

What they ask

Canvas page 1.4 has two parts. Part A is drawing practice: place unit icons on a flowsheet, connect them with streams, insert a mixer, resize and rotate icons, delete and redraw. Part B is the first real run: unpack Ex10-1Screen.PAK (King's worked example 10.1, a screening problem), run it, look at every stream, and export the flowsheet and the particle size distributions the way the course wants them in reports. Nothing is graded; everything later assumes you did it.

How the work flows

This is a tutorial, and it has no loop. Nothing is designed and nothing is tuned: the PAK arrives with its material, its screen and its flowsheet already defined, and the work is to look at each of the three from the result side (the fly-outs and the unit report), then to export the two figures the way the course wants them in every later report. Part A, the drawing practice, runs nothing at all.

  • Fixed for every run: the unpacked job as King built it: one feed stream, one screen with the Karra model SCR2, an oversize and an undersize product stream, the material and the size classes inside the PAK. You type no data.
  • Chosen once: the work area and the decimal point of page 1.3 (classic MODSIM), and the name you save the job under, so the original PAK stays as it came.
  • Read, nothing turned: the fly-out of each of the three streams (solids flow, water flow, per cent solids, size distribution), the screen's unit report (its separation and its area utilisation factor), and the system data (size classes, the mineral and its density) where the program shows them.
  • Compared at the end: the two products against the feed, the undersize against the aperture, King's Table 10.1 and his own result against your run, the exported figures at 400 % zoom in the document, and SolEx_B.doc after your own attempt.

In the page's words, as What they ask transcribes them, "draw" (Part A) produces nothing to keep; "unpack", "run" and "look at every stream" (Part B) are the reading; "export" is the only deliverable, and the reason the exercise exists.

How to check the run

The Canvas page gives no numbers; the PAK and King's example do.

  • Mass. The feed fly-out shows the tonnage the PAK carries: 25.063 in the file's own unit, kg/s, which is 90.2 t/h, the 99.4 short tons per hour of King's Figure 10.3; oversize plus undersize must give it back to the decimal. A gap means the PAK did not unpack cleanly, or you are reading a stream of Part A's scratch drawing.
  • Size. The aperture is 1.25 inch, 1.25×25.4=31.75 mm (King's Table 10.1). The undersize must be entirely finer than the aperture: 100 % passing 31.75 mm. The oversize is not entirely coarser: in King's test 40.6 % of it still passed 1.25 inch, because the screen was overloaded, and the Karra model reproduces that tail.
  • The report. King's own run gave an area utilisation factor of 1.58: the test screen was somewhat overloaded (printed page 351). Your unit report should say the same; a factor near 1 would mean a different deck size was typed.
  • The figures. Zoom to 400 % in Word: a vector figure (EMF, or the GUI's export) stays sharp; a screenshot pixelates.

Why (the engineering)

The exercise is small on purpose. A simulator is three descriptions glued together (material, machines, flowsheet), and the fastest way to see how MODSIM stores each of them is to open a job somebody else built and click through it: where the size classes live, where the feed is defined, what a unit report contains, and how a stream carries solids, water and a size distribution at once. The exporting part matters because every assignment presentation needs a flowsheet with fly-outs and a size-distribution plot, and the PostScript-to-EMF route is where people lose an evening the night before a seminar.

Before MODSIM: numbers by hand

Read King's example 10.1 (section 10.4, printed page 350) first. Its Table 10.1 gives the screen and the test: a 16 ft × 0.88 ft woven-wire deck, 1.25 inch aperture, 0.5 inch wire, 20° inclination, dolomite at 110 lb/ft³, and the measured feed, oversize and undersize curves. Write three things down before you open the PAK:

  • the aperture in MODSIM's unit: 1.25×25.4=31.75 mm, that is 0.03175 m;
  • the bulk density in MODSIM's unit: 110×16.02=1762 kg/m³, the sixth SCR2 parameter;
  • the feed tonnage and the size classes, which are in Figure 10.4 (the system data form) and in the PAK itself: 25.063 kg/s at 100 % solids (90.2 t/h; King's figure shows it as 99.4 short tons per hour), dolomite at 2.7 t/m³, 12 size classes from 76 mm down in a 2 series.

When the run finishes, the fly-out of the feed must show that tonnage to the decimal and the products must add up to it. If they do not, either the PAK did not unpack cleanly or you are looking at the wrong stream. Two numbers, checked before anything else, save the whole afternoon.

Here is the job as the PAK carries it, with the stream names it uses; the fly-outs of these three streams are the whole of Part B.

Expected results

The products of the screen sum to the feed tonnage; the oversize is coarser than the aperture except for the near-size leakage a real screen model produces, and here that leakage is large because King's test screen was overloaded (area utilisation factor 1.58); the size-distribution plot shows three curves (feed, oversize, undersize) with the undersize entirely finer than the aperture. The worked solution SolEx_B.doc in the module 1 material shows what Part B should look like: open it after your own run.

Common mistakes

Watch out

Working inside the program folder. Classic MODSIM throws its swarm of job files wherever the work area points; without the MODSIM variable that is Program Files, where it gets lost or write-protected.

Also: a decimal comma; unpacking two jobs into the same work folder so that files overwrite each other; forgetting that closing without packing leaves nothing portable; PNG screenshots of the flowsheet in the report instead of vector EMF.

  • Treating the run as the result. The run takes a second; the exercise is the reading (three fly-outs, the report, the system data) and the export. Closing MODSIM with the numbers unread and a screenshot in hand is how this tutorial gets redone the night before Assignment 1.

Sensitivity analysis

None is asked. If you want one: change the screen aperture in the unpacked job by 10 % up and down and watch how the two products move; it is a two-minute preview of Exercise 1.
