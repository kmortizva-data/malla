Exercise 4: open and closed tertiary circuit

module 2 · King 5.1-5.6 (Table 5.7), 4 (Karra)

What they ask

Secondary-crushed Kiruna D-ore, ρs 4.0 t/m³, 600 t/h, size distribution RRS with d63.2 = 15 mm and n = 0.9 in 20 fractions. Work index for crushing and screening Wi = 12 kWh/t. Model names annotated beside each machine; fly-outs on feed and product discharges.

  1. Open circuit: a tertiary crushing stage that delivers a feed for primary grinding with d80 ≈ 5 mm and/or d99 ≈ 10 mm, without any risk of choked crushing. Crusher and model are your choice; the screen must be a Karra model.
  2. Closed circuit: the same target, with the crusher discharge screened and the oversize returned.
  3. Analysis: plot the two feeds to primary grinding; compute the total energy in kWh/t including a primary mill that discharges at d80 = 0.1 mm; ask whether the machines you chose exist.

Why (the engineering)

Open or closed is a question about what the mill downstream can tolerate. A rod mill or an undemanding grind accepts the tail of an open circuit; a primary ball mill wants a controlled top size, so the crusher discharge is screened and the oversize goes round again. The closed circuit buys a guaranteed d99 at the price of a recirculating load: the crusher and the screen must both carry the fresh feed plus what comes back.

Energy is the second axis, and the sheet states the rule of thumb: crush as far as you can, because a millimetre removed in a crusher costs far less than the same millimetre removed in a mill. You will see it in your own numbers: the mill term of the Bond calculation dominates the total, and every reduction of the mill feed d80 takes a visible bite out of it. Throughput and equipment size then decide what is practical, which is why the last question is whether your crusher exists in a catalogue.

"No risk of choked crushing" means the crusher must be able to pass what you feed it at that CSS: check the capacity for your CSS in King's Table 5.7 (short-head cones) or 5.6 (standard cones) against the tonnage the flowsheet sends it, including the recirculation in the closed case.

Before MODSIM: numbers by hand

The feed. P(d) = 1 − exp(−(d/15)^{0.9}):

Size (mm)25101525.54082
Cum % finer14.831.050.163.2809199

Half of the feed is already finer than 10 mm and a third finer than 5 mm. That is the whole design idea in one line: screen first, so that only the coarse half goes to the crusher. The feed d80 is 25.5 mm, the d99 about 82 mm.

Flows if the screen were perfect. A 10 mm cut sends about 300 t/h straight to the mill feed and about 300 t/h to the crusher. A real Karra screen at 10 mm passes a little less (near-size particles stay on the deck), so expect roughly 285 t/h undersize and 315 t/h to the crusher in the open case.

Energy by Bond. W = 10·Wi·(1/√P80 − 1/√F80) with the sizes in micrometres. The mill, from a mill feed d80 of 5 mm to 0.1 mm: W = 10 × 12 × (1/√100 − 1/√5000) = 120 × (0.1000 − 0.0141) = 10.3 kWh/t. The crusher stage, from about 25 mm to about 7 mm on the tonnes it actually crushes: 120 × (1/√7000 − 1/√25500) ≈ 0.68 kWh per crushed tonne, which is about 0.36 kWh per tonne of circuit feed in open circuit. The mill is fifteen to thirty times the crusher; that ratio is the engineering answer to "why crush as far as possible".

Why

Circulating load. In the closed circuit the crusher feed is the fresh oversize plus the recirculated oversize of the second screen. If the crusher discharge has, say, 20 % coarser than the aperture, the recirculation settles around 20 % of the fresh oversize and the crusher runs at about 1.2 times the open-circuit tonnage. Put that tonnage, not the fresh one, against the capacity table.

Metres in the GUI: 100 mm is 0.1, 10 mm is 0.010, 5 mm is 0.005

Feed 600 t/h, RRS d63.2 15 mm, n 0.9

ClickFeed stream > 600 t/h > density 4.0 > cumulative % finer from RRS.xls at your class sizes
Expect50 % at 10 mm, 63.2 % at 15 mm, 80 % at 25.5 mm
3

Open circuit: Karra screen, crusher on the oversize, mixer to the mill feed

ClickFeed > Screen (SCR2, Karra) aperture 10 mm. Oversize > Crusher (SHHD, CSS to be tuned). Screen undersize and crusher product > Mixer > product "Mill feed". Annotate SCR2 and SHHD beside the icons; fly-outs on feed, screen products, crusher product, mill feed
ExpectUndersize near 285 t/h, crusher feed near 315 t/h
WhySHHD is the tertiary (short-head) model; the sheet leaves the crusher free but wants the screen to be Karra
The mixer is what makes "mill feed" one stream; without it you are comparing two half-products
4

Tune the crusher setting until the mill feed meets d80 5 mm and/or d99 10 mm

ClickSHHD: closed-side setting start at 0.008 m, K 0.2, Wi 12, alpha1 0.653, alpha2 2. Run. Read d80 and d99 of the mill feed in its report. Adjust the CSS and rerun
ExpectA CSS between about 6 and 10 mm gets the d80 to 5 mm; the d99 target is the hard one in open circuit because whatever the crusher lets through coarse goes straight to the mill
WhyThis is the open circuit's weakness made visible: the d99 is set by the crusher's tail, not by a screen
5

Check choked crushing against the capacity table

ClickRead the crusher feed tonnage from its fly-out. Open Tables5.4-5.7.pdf, Table 5.7 (short-head cones): find a machine whose capacity at your CSS covers the tonnage
ExpectA real machine size and a margin; if no short-head cone reaches the tonnage at that CSS, split into two crushers in parallel or open the CSS
Why"Without any risk of choked crushing" is a capacity statement. MODSIM does not check it for you (Exercise 3 showed you that)
6

Closed circuit: return the coarse tail to the crusher

ClickCopy the flowsheet. Crusher product > Screen 2 (SCR2, same aperture). Screen 2 undersize > Mixer (mill feed). Screen 2 oversize > Mixer in front of the crusher, together with Screen 1 oversize. Run
ExpectMODSIM iterates the recycle loop; the crusher feed rises by the recirculation (roughly a fifth of the fresh oversize), and the mill feed d99 now sits at the aperture
WhyThe second screen guarantees the top size; the crusher only has to bring the d80 down
Watch the convergence message. If the loop does not converge, the circulating load is running away: open the CSS or the aperture
7

Energy, and the plot

ClickUnit report of each crusher: power kW. Divide by the circuit feed (600 t/h) for kWh per tonne of feed. Mill energy by Bond by hand from the mill feed d80 to 100 micrometres with Wi 12. Plot the two mill-feed curves with the raw feed in one diagram
ExpectMill term around 10 kWh/t, crusher term well under 1 kWh/t; the closed circuit's mill feed the finer of the two
WhyThe comparison the sheet wants is total kWh/t, so both terms must be on the same basis: per tonne of circuit feed

Common mistakes

Watch out

Bond with millimetres. The 10 in W = 10 Wi (1/sqrt(P80) - 1/sqrt(F80)) belongs to sizes in micrometres. With millimetres the mill energy comes out 30 times too large. Convert first: 5 mm = 5000 um, 0.1 mm = 100 um.

Watch out

Choke checked against the fresh feed. In closed circuit the crusher sees the recirculation as well; read the crusher fly-out after the loop converged and check THAT tonnage against Table 5.7.

Also: an aperture larger than the d99 target (the mill then receives what the screen was meant to hold back); the recycle drawn to the crusher directly instead of through a mixer; forgetting the second screen's model annotation; a size mesh whose finest class is too coarse to resolve d80 = 5 mm; reporting kWh per tonne of crusher feed and per tonne of circuit feed in the same table without saying which.
