Assignment 1, step by step: the crusher station

What they ask

The sheet is Assignment_1.pdf (Bertil Pålsson, 2013-10-10, two pages). Its purpose, in its own words, is to train the building and simulation of a crusher station for dry products, in three parts: build your own circuit, simulate it with simple models, simulate it again with advanced models. Here is the task, point by point, in the order of the sheet.

1. The reference plant (section 2). USIM PAC's example case5 is an aggregate plant (gravel and sand for construction) with two impact crushers, one autogenous crusher (a VSI) and a set of screens. You only care about the part that makes the sized products 0-40, 0-20 and 40-70 mm, the intermediate fractions 10-20 and 4-10 mm, and the rest fraction 0-4 mm. The figure on page 1 is the whole flowsheet with numbered streams (1 to 40) and circled unit numbers. In that figure the two impact crushers are the units labelled Primary Crusher ④ and Secondary Crusher ⑭; the autogenous crusher is the Barmac ⑩.

2. The material (section 3.1), fixed by the sheet:

GivenValue
Size fractions in the model20
Largest particle600 mm
Feed distributionRRS with d'=150 mm and n (lambda) =1.26
Moisture2 %
Feed rate300 t/h

3. The flowsheet simplifications (section 3.2), all five mandatory:

  • a) screens ① and ② are one double-deck screen;
  • b) process streams 10 and 21 are removed;
  • c) process stream 31 directly gives the 0-4 mm fraction;
  • d) the two impact crushers and the VSI are replaced by crushers you select;
  • e) splitter ⑧ is adjustable, default 50 %; the other splitter likewise.

4. Anything else (section 3.3): make the assumptions and simplifications you need, and say which.

5. Simple models (section 4): run a simulation that produces the wanted fractions without overloading crushers or screens; present a flowsheet with flyouts, the particle size distributions of the products (and of any stream you consider essential), and condensed machine data for every unit.

6. Advanced models (section 5): give the circuit a new name and save it under that name first; switch the screens to the Karra model; no screen larger than 3 × 6 m; replace the crusher models with more advanced ones; take the crusher sizes from the tables in King chapter 5 and check that each machine can take its feed and that setting, capacity and power are realistic and within the manufacturer's recommendation; run again and compare with the simple-model result.

Note

The course's rule 0.8 on AI applies to the report: language help is allowed and must be declared; the analysis, the results and the conclusions are the group's own. This guide explains what the sheet asks and how to organise the work. It does not give you a circuit or a set of results, on purpose.

How the work flows

The sheet reads like a list of settings, so the natural misreading is: define the material, press Run, save the report, then swap a few machines and compare. That is not the job. The assignment is a design loop, run twice, and the report is the record of the loop. Four things have different roles, and keeping them apart is most of the work:

  • Fixed for every run: the material of section 3.1, the topology (case5 with the five simplifications) and the six products. If any of these changes between two runs, the runs cannot be compared.
  • Chosen once, before the first run: the type of crusher in each of the three places. Where case5 has its primary impact crusher you put a jaw or a gyratory; where it has the secondary impact crusher and the Barmac you put cones. This is sheet item 3.2 d.
  • Turned in part 4: the knobs of the simple models. Crusher settings (open-side setting for the primary, closed-side setting for the cones), the aperture of each screen deck and the two splitter percentages. You turn one knob, run, read, turn the next.
  • Changed in part 5: the models, not the circuit. Karra screens that need a real deck size, cycle-model crushers that need a real machine size from King's tables. Settings and apertures stay as in part 4 unless a machine limit forces a change, and then you say so.

In the sheet's own words, "select" happens twice: crusher types in part 4, machine sizes in part 5. "Evaluate" also happens twice: does the circuit make the six fractions without overloading anything (part 4); are the machines realistic and inside the manufacturer's tables (part 5). The comparison of the two, explained in process terms, is the discussion the rubric pays for.

How to check overloading

The sheet says "without overloading crushers and screens" and does not say how to tell. The simple models cannot tell you: an ideal screen passes whatever you feed it and CRS1 crushes any tonnage you send. So in part 4 the check is yours, made by hand, and how you made it belongs in the assumption list (sheet 3.3, rubric criterion 1).

  • Crushers. From the fly-out of each crusher take the tonnage and the top size of its feed. Open King's Table 5.4 (jaw, by open-side setting), 5.5 (gyratory, by open-side setting), 5.6 (standard cone, by closed-side setting) or 5.7 (short-head cone); the same tables are on Canvas as Tables5.4-5.7.pdf and, legibly, on pages 152 to 155 of the King e-book. Three questions, always in this order: does the machine swallow the feed (King, section 5.6.1: the largest lump at most about 80 % of the gape for jaws and gyratories; for cones the closed-side feed opening larger than the largest lump); does it allow your setting (a column with a number in that row; for cones the recommended minimum CSS); does it take your tonnage (down the column of your setting to the first row whose capacity covers the fly-out's tonnage). That row is the machine, with its motor power. If no row at your setting reaches your tonnage, the crusher is overloaded: open the setting, send it less (the splitters exist for that) or plan two identical machines in parallel behind a splitter.
  • Worked reading for the primary. The primary receives the +40 mm of the double deck, roughly 250 t/h with lumps up to 600 mm. A gyratory needs a gape of at least 600 / 0.8 = 750 mm, about 30 inches: the smallest gyratory of Table 5.5, the 30 × 60 (150 kW), gives 313 t/h at OSS 51 mm, 450 at 76, 567 at 102 and 760 at 140, so it takes 250 t/h at any setting, and the choice of setting becomes a product-size decision you can motivate. A jaw of the same gape, the 30 × 42 of Table 5.4 (75 kW), gives 227 t/h at OSS 127 mm and 272 at 152: it only fits with a coarse product; the 36 × 48 (93 kW) gives 300 t/h at OSS 127. One sentence saying why the jaw has to be bigger than the gyratory for the same duty is the kind of thing the seminar rewards. The tertiary cone of the Exercise 4 guide is read the same way from Table 5.7.
  • Screens in part 4. An ideal screen has no area, so either assume a deck size and check the load per square metre with the screening slides of module 2 (ScreenFormulas.pdf, Screens.xls), or state that screen loading is checked in part 5, where the model gives the number. Both are legitimate; only silence is not.
  • Part 5. Karra reports an area utilisation per deck; above 1 the deck is smaller than its load, and the sheet caps you at 3 × 6 m, so the honest answers are two screens in parallel, a different split upstream or reporting the overload as a finding. The cycle crushers take a real machine size, but MODSIM does not refuse an overload: the comparison with the table row is still yours, and the seminar will ask for it.

Key points

The rubric has five criteria of 5 points each (from the Canvas assignment page): problem definition and assumptions · flowsheet and material definition · model selection and parameters · simulation results · discussion and analysis. Read the sheet against that grid and the priorities appear:

  1. Select and evaluate, in that order, twice. Types and knobs, then the six-fraction and overload check (part 4); models and sizes, then the table check (part 5). A run that nobody evaluated is not a result, and a report that shows only the last run has thrown away the evidence of the loop.
  2. Assumptions are a deliverable, not a footnote. Section 3.3 invites you to assume; criterion 1 pays for stating what you assumed and why (which crusher types, which screen apertures, what "overloaded" means for you, what the splitters do, what happens to the 20-40 mm that case5 sends towards the 0/20 product).
  3. The feed defines the job. With d'=150 mm and n=1.26, the RRS function P(d)=1−exp⁡[−(d/d')n] says only about 17 % of the feed is finer than 40 mm and 1 % finer than 4 mm: nearly everything you sell has to be made by the crushers, not screened out of the feed. The size classes, the top size (0.6 m, not 0.01 m) and the moisture (98 % solids) must be entered right or every later number is wrong.
  4. Simple models are the baseline, not the answer. Ideal screens (SCRN, DSC1: aperture and a transmission efficiency) and simple crushers (JAW1, GYRA, CRS1: a setting and a standard product curve) let you find a circuit that makes the six fractions. Keep that run: part 5 compares against it, and "How the work flows" above says what may change between the two.
  5. The advanced round is where grades 4 and 5 live. Karra screens (SCR2, DSC2) need real deck dimensions, so the 3 × 6 m limit bites; cycle-model crushers (CRSH, SHHD) need real machine sizes from King's Tables 5.4 to 5.7, checked against capacity and power at the chosen setting. The comparison, explained in process terms (why the Karra screens misplace material, why the cone's product is coarser or finer, where the power went), is the discussion the rubric rewards.
  6. "Condensed machine data" is one table. Per unit: model code, setting (OSS or CSS, aperture), size, feed tonnage, power, and for screens the area utilisation. Decide early who fills it and from which MODSIM report.
  7. The seminar is part of the grade. Every machine choice will be questioned by the opposing group; two people should be able to defend each one with a number (tonnage, setting, table row).

2

Draw the simplified circuit on paper from the case5 figure

ClickWith the map above and the original figure side by side, draw your own version: feed, the double-deck screen, the primary crusher, screens 70/40/20, the two splitters, the secondary and tertiary crushers, screens 20/10/4, and the six products with the stream numbers of the figure. The two impact crushers are the units the figure labels Primary Crusher ④ and Secondary Crusher ⑭; the VSI is the Barmac ⑩. Put a crusher unit of your own choice in each of the three places (a jaw or a gyratory for the primary, cones for the other two) and write your choice next to it.
ExpectA sketch with three crushers, seven screens (one of them double deck), two splitters, the mixers you need, and six labelled product streams: 0/40, 40/70, 0/20, 10/20, 4/10 and 0/4.
WhyBuilding in MODSIM from a paper sketch is faster, and the sketch becomes the report's figure 1 and the caption of your assumption list.
Mark which streams are products and which return to a crusher: the recirculation (+70 and half of the 40-70 to the secondary, +20 back to the secondary, half of the 20-40 to the tertiary) is what can overload a machine. And decide now what your 0/20 product is, because as drawn in case5 half of the 20-40 mm ends up in it.
3

Define the material in MODSIM exactly as the sheet says

ClickNew project, then the Material Definition wizard: one material, 20 size classes with the largest at 0.6 m, feed 300 t/h at 98 % solids, particle size distribution from the RRS with d' 150 mm and n 1.26 (RRS.xls gives the sieve points).
ExpectThe feed stream shows about 17 % finer than 40 mm, 32 % finer than 70 mm and 63 % finer than 150 mm.
WhyThese three numbers are the check that the RRS was entered right.
The wizard works in metres for the size classes and in micrometres in the PSD dialog; the Exercise 1 guide shows every window.
4

Build part 4 with the simple models, unit by unit from the table

ClickPlace the units of your sketch and give each the part-4 model of the table above: DSC1 for the double deck (apertures 100 and 40 mm), SCRN for the six single decks (70, 40, 20, 20, 10, 4 mm) with a transmission efficiency (90 % is the usual first guess), JAW1 or GYRA for the primary with an open-side setting, CRS1 for the secondary and the tertiary with a closed-side setting each, both splitters at 50 %. Name the three crushers primary, secondary and tertiary, and name every stream (product 0/40, recycle to secondary, ...).
ExpectThe flowsheet runs, the fly-outs show a tonnage in every product stream, and the Review has no errors.
WhyThe simple models have few parameters, so the first run tells you whether the topology and the settings make the six fractions at all. It is the starting point of the loop, not a result.
Unnamed streams make the fly-outs unreadable and the Review warns about duplicates. Three crushers, not two: the Barmac was the third and it is replaced too.
5

Tune the simple circuit until nothing is overloaded

ClickOne knob per run: a setting, a deck aperture or a splitter percentage. After each run read the six product tonnages and, for each crusher and each deck, the tonnage it carries; check the crushers against King's table row for their setting as "How to check overloading" describes, and write one line in a run log: what you changed, what moved.
ExpectThe six fractions right, a table of their tonnages, and for each unit a tonnage that a real machine of that type takes at that setting. The run log is the raw material of the discussion.
Why"Without overloading" is the sheet's own success criterion for part 4, and the sheet leaves the check to you.
Two knobs in one run and you will not know which one did it. If a crusher never fits a table row at any setting, the answer is upstream (send it less with the splitters) or two machines, not a bigger number in the fly-out.
6

Save, export and fill the machine table for part 4

ClickSave as A1_simple.PAK. Export the flowsheet with fly-outs and the PSD plots of the product streams (and of the crusher discharges if they help). Fill the condensed machine data table.
ExpectFigures and a table that could go straight into the report.
WhyPart 4 must survive untouched: part 5 compares against it.
Keep the PAK of part 4 as it is; part 5 starts from a copy with a new name (that is sheet item 5a).
7

Start part 5 under a new name and switch the screens to Karra

ClickSave as A1_advanced.PAK. Change each screen to SCR2 (single deck) or DSC2 (double deck) and enter deck dimensions no larger than 3 × 6 m, the apertures and the wire diameters.
ExpectThe Karra run reports an area utilisation for each deck; anything far above 1 means the deck is too small for the load.
WhyThe Karra model computes the separation from the deck's real size and load, which is why the 3 × 6 m limit changes the answer.
If a single 3 × 6 m deck cannot take the load, that is a finding to report (two screens in parallel, or a different split), not an error to hide.
8

Replace the crushers with the cycle models and size them from King

ClickChange the primary to CRSH (gyratory or jaw cycle model) and the secondary and tertiary to CRSH (standard cone) or SHHD (short head). For each, pick a machine from Tables 5.4 to 5.7: the row whose capacity at your setting covers your tonnage, whose feed opening takes your feed's top size, with the power the table gives.
ExpectFor every crusher: model code, machine size, setting, capacity at that setting, feed opening, installed power, and your simulated tonnage inside the range.
WhySheet item 5e is scored on exactly this check, and the seminar will ask for it.
Tables give capacity per setting column; read the column of your setting, not the largest number in the row. Keep the part-4 settings unless a table forces a change, and then say so in the report.
9

Run, compare and explain

ClickRun the advanced circuit. Build one comparison table: for each product and each unit, part 4 versus part 5 (tonnage, d80 or the fraction limits, screen utilisation, crusher power).
ExpectDifferences you can explain in process terms: where the Karra screens misplace fines, how the cycle model changes the crusher product, what the machine limits did to the circuit.
WhySheet item 5f and rubric criterion 5 are the same thing: the comparison is the analysis.
Compare like with like: same feed, same apertures and settings unless a machine limit forced a change, and then say so.
10

Write the report against the rubric and rehearse the defence

ClickFive sections, one per criterion, in the rubric's order; the assumption list first; the machine table and the comparison table as the core; the AI-use declaration if any help was used.
ExpectA report where every number has a unit and a source (which run, which table row), and two group members who can defend each machine choice.
WhyThe seminar on Friday 18 September (derived date) includes opposing another group; the questions you prepare for are the ones you will ask them.

Deliverables checklist

Common mistakes

  • Treating the first run as the result. The assignment is the loop; the report shows the evidence of the loop, not only its last frame.
  • Replacing two crushers of three: the Barmac is the VSI of item 3.2 d and it goes too.
  • Reading the figure with oversize and undersize swapped, so that the products come out of the wrong side of every screen.
  • Size class top of 0.01 m instead of 0.6 m, and 100 % solids instead of 98: the feed is then not the sheet's feed (the Exercise 1 file had both slips).
  • Changing the screen and crusher models in the part-4 file instead of a renamed copy: the comparison of item 5f becomes impossible.
  • Karra decks larger than 3 × 6 m because the utilisation looked better: the sheet forbids it; report the overload instead.
  • A crusher size chosen for its capacity at the wrong setting, or a capacity read in the wrong unit (t/h against m³/h).
  • Fly-outs and plots without stream names, and tables without units.
  • Discussion that lists numbers without saying what the numbers mean for the plant.
  • Assumptions made in MODSIM but never written down.
