Exercise 1: design of a screening station

What they ask

Kirunavaara D-ore comes out of the cobbing plant at 600 t/h, solids density 4.0 t/m³, with the size distribution below. Build a screening station that splits it into three fractions: coarser than 100 mm, 20 to 100 mm, and finer than 20 mm. Two product streams leave the station:

  • the middle fraction (20 to 100 mm) goes to possible extra crushing;
  • the coarse and the fines together feed the autogenous (AG) mills.

Use the Karra screen models, annotate every product stream, and put fly-outs on all streams. Then a sensitivity run: move the deck that limits the middle fraction on the coarse side in 5 mm steps up and down and see what happens to screen areas, the d80, d50 and d20 of the products, and the mass flows. Plot it in Excel.

Why (the engineering)

Why three fractions and not two. An AG mill grinds the ore with the ore: the biggest lumps are the grinding media. The sheet says a mill needs roughly 15 wt-% coarser than 100 mm in its feed to work. But lumps of about 20 to 50 mm are the wrong size: too small to grind anything, too big to be ground quickly. They accumulate as critical stones and eat mill capacity. So the station removes the range that would become critical stones (the middle fraction, sent to crushing) and keeps both the media (coarse) and what is already fine.

Why two cut sizes means two decks. Three fractions need two separations: one at 100 mm and one at 20 mm. In MODSIM that is either two single-deck screens in series (the undersize of the 100 mm screen feeds the 20 mm screen) or one double-deck screen. Two single decks let you size each deck's area separately, which is exactly what the sensitivity question asks about.

Why Karra and not a distribution curve. A distribution-curve model (CYCB) takes a partition curve you give it and applies it whatever the feed rate: it cannot tell you the area you need. The Karra model computes the separation from the basic capacity with corrections for near-size material, oversize load, open area and so on. It responds to loading, so it can answer "how big must the screen be" and "what happens if I move the cut". That is the whole point of the exercise.

Why the coarse plus fines stream still works as AG feed. Check it before you simulate: if about 94 t/h are coarser than 100 mm and about 254 t/h finer than 20 mm (numbers below), the combined stream carries roughly 94 / 348 = 27 wt-% coarser than 100 mm, above the 15 % the mill wants. If your simulation shows much less, something is sending coarse rock to the wrong place.

Before MODSIM: numbers by hand

Ideal separation is the upper bound. Read the feed table in What they ask as cumulative percent finer:

  • Coarser than 100 mm: 100 − 84.3 = 15.7 % → 0.157 × 600 = 94 t/h.
  • Finer than 20 mm: 20 mm is not in the table. Interpolate on a log size axis between 15 mm (36.9 %) and 30 mm (49.9 %): ln(20/15) / ln(30/15) = 0.415, so 36.9 + 0.415 × 13.0 = 42.3 % → 254 t/h.
  • Middle, 20 to 100 mm: 84.3 − 42.3 = 42.0 % → 252 t/h.

So the ideal station sends about 252 t/h to crushing and 348 t/h to the AG circuit. A real Karra screen will not hit these exactly: near-size particles (roughly 0.75 to 1.25 times the aperture) partly report to the oversize, so expect the coarse products a few percent heavier and slightly contaminated with undersize, and the fine products a little lighter and cleaner. If your simulated flows are far from these, check the feed definition first.

Why

The d80 of the whole feed is about 90 mm (84.3 % passes 100 mm, 73.5 % passes 70 mm). The d50 is about 30 mm. Keep those two in mind: after a good station, the AG feed should have a d80 clearly above 100 mm and the crusher feed a d80 around 70 to 90 mm.

Step by step in MODSIM

Before the first click: Get MODSIM running without grief covers the decimal point, the work area and the PAK habits. Your Feed Stream dialog showing 600,0000 with a comma is the symptom it fixes. The course accepts both programs; the steps below follow the MODSIM-GUI material wizard tab by tab, with the classic MODSIM equivalent in one line. Each "How to fill this window" fold lists the values field by field and shows the window as it looked on 4 September, before the values went in.

1

Draw the flowsheet and name the two products

ClickGUI: File > New project; place Screen-1, Screen-2 and Mixer-3; draw Feed > Screen-1, Screen-1 undersize > Screen-2, Screen-1 oversize > Mixer-3, Screen-2 undersize > Mixer-3, Screen-2 oversize > product named "To crushing", Mixer-3 > product named "AG feed". Classic: File > Start a new job, same drawing
ExpectSix streams: one feed, three internal, two products, each product carrying its name (the sheet demands annotations on every product stream)
WhyThe material wizard lists the streams it finds on the drawing; an unnamed product shows up as "Screen-2 → ?" and two of them raise the "duplicate stream name" warning
In classic MODSIM a stream takes its meaning from WHERE it touches the unit icon: the oversize and undersize ports are different spots. Right-click the stream to confirm which port it is on
2

Material wizard, Components tab: one ore, its density, material type Mineral

ClickFlowsheet > Material Definition (System Data) > Components
Expect"No issues detected", and the Review tab stops warning that the material type is not selected
WhyScreening is, in the course's words, a dry process without mineral specification: one solid phase carries the whole flow and its density (4.0 t/m³ on the sheet) only enters the volumetric checks. Classic MODSIM: system parameters, one mineral, SPGR 4.0
Press Save before closing the wizard; a half-filled tab is forgotten with the window
How to fill this window
  • Material type = Mineral
  • Add mineral, Name = Kirunavaara D-ore
  • Specific gravity g/cm³ = 4
  • Magnetic susceptibility κ = 0 (not used by a screen)
  • Save, then Next
3

Grades, Properties, Liberation, Washability: pass through

ClickNext, four times
ExpectEvery tab says "No issues detected" or "Proceed to next step"
WhyGrades, S-classes, liberation and washability describe assays, density classes, mineral texture and coal float-sink curves: nothing a screen needs. They return in modules 3 to 6
How to fill this window
  • Grades = one particle grade class, basis Mass, 1.0 for the ore, sp. gr. 4.000 filled by the wizard
  • Properties = "Use S-classes" unticked
  • Liberation = "Specify liberation models of Andrews-Mika diagram" unticked
  • Washability = nothing to do (coal float-sink only)
4

Size_Conv. tab: a size mesh that covers 600 mm

ClickSize_Conv. > Largest particle size
ExpectThe Review summary reads "Largest particle size: 0.9 m" and the PSD later fits without a top-size warning
WhyMODSIM only tracks mass in the classes you define. Your file had 0.01 m here: a 10 mm top class throws 70 % of this feed into the top bin and every screen downstream computes nonsense. A √2 series of 25 classes from 0.9 m reaches 0.22 mm, plenty for cuts at 100 and 20 mm
This field is in metres while the PSD table is in micrometres: 0.9 m here, 600000 µm there. Write the unit next to every number you type
How to fill this window
  • Largest particle size = 0.9 (METRES; the course's worked example CobbScreen.PAK uses 0.9 m)
  • Number of size classes = 25
  • Convergence method = Modified Newton (default)
  • Tolerance required = 0.1 % (default)
  • Maximum iterations allowed = 30
  • Start simulation from previous end point = ticked
5

Streams tab: define the feed

ClickStreams > Feed Streams > row 1 (→ Screen-1) > Material = Feed > open the row's Feed Stream dialog
ExpectThe feed row shows 600 t/h, 100 %, "Manual (10 pts)" and status OK; "Top Size Fraction (auto)" reports a raw PSD max of 600000 µm; the process streams stay "Default" because MODSIM computes them
WhyEverything downstream is arithmetic on this curve. A feed left at 0 t/h runs without an error and every stream shows zeros: that was the state of your file on 2 September
Windows with a decimal comma shows "600,0000" in this dialog. Fix the regional setting first (setup guide); otherwise the engine may read the number as garbage
How to fill this window
  • Solids feed rate = 600 tonnes/hr
  • Percent Solid = 100 (dry feed; the worked example CobbScreen.PAK feeds 600 t/h at 100.00 % solids)
  • Particle Size Distribution = Manual > Define PSD… > the ten sieve points of the sheet in µm with cumulative % finer: 600000 → 99.5, 300000 → 95.1, 100000 → 84.3, 70000 → 73.5, 50000 → 64.1, 30000 → 49.9, 15000 → 36.9, 10000 → 30.0, 5000 → 19.8, 3300 → 15.1
  • Specify grade distributions = unticked
  • Specify distribution over S-classes = unticked
  • OK
6

Review tab: zero errors, then Confirm & Finish

ClickReview
Expect"Status: OK" and a summary with Minerals 1, Size classes 25, Largest particle size 0.9 m
WhyThe Review tab is the wizard's own checklist; each line names the tab where the fix lives (the table in the setup guide decodes them)
How to fill this window
  • Errors = 0
  • Warnings = 0 (material type selected, feed defined, products named)
  • Confirm & Finish
7

Give both screens the Karra model and their apertures

ClickScreen-1 > model SCR2 (single-deck Karra) > aperture 0.100 m. Screen-2 > SCR2 > aperture 0.020 m. Classic: same codes in the unit-models menu
ExpectThe model asks for the deck dimensions and the open area besides the aperture; start with a plausible deck (a few metres by a few metres) and let the report tell you how loaded it is
WhyThe Karra model computes the separation from capacity and load; the area you give it is a design variable, not a fixed input
The default aperture in the GUI is 0.0508 m (2 inches, an American default). Leaving it means both screens cut at 51 mm and the station makes no sense
8

Run, then read the fly-outs and the unit reports

ClickRun the simulation; switch fly-outs on for every stream; open the screen reports
ExpectFlows near 94 / 252 / 254 t/h for the three fractions; each screen report shows its basic capacity, correction factors and area utilisation
WhyThe report is where "required screen area" lives: if the utilisation is far above 1 the deck is overloaded, far below 1 it is oversized
Fly-outs show solids flow, water flow, % solids and analysis. In a dry job water is zero by construction; that is fine here
9

Save, and save the sensitivity variants under new names

ClickGUI: save the .mpd in Projects MODSIM and export a PAK. Classic: pack the job to a .PAK
ExpectFiles like Ex1_base, Ex1_cut85, Ex1_cut90 … in Projects MODSIM
WhyMODSIM's work folder is a swarm of small files; the PAK is what you keep, share and submit

Common mistakes

These first three are not hypothetical: they are in the Excercise 1.mpd saved on 2 September, and they are the fastest lesson this exercise has.

Watch out

Both screens still at the default aperture of 0.0508 m. The station cuts twice at 51 mm: there is no 100 mm split and no 20 mm split. Set 0.100 m and 0.020 m.

Watch out

The size mesh stops at 0.01 m (10 mm) while the feed reaches 600 mm. Seventy percent of the feed lands in the top class and cannot be screened. Extend the top size to 1.0 m before entering the feed.

Watch out

The feed stream has the mineral and a density of 4.0 but "defined: false" and a feed rate of 0 t/h. The job runs and every stream is empty. Enter 600 t/h and the ten sieve points.

What the wizard's Review tab told you on 4 September, and where each line is fixed: Material type is not selected → Components tab, pick Mineral. Feed stream "? → Screen-1" has no material definition → Streams tab, open the feed row and press OK on the Feed Stream dialog. Duplicate stream name: Screen-2 → ? → name the two product streams on the flowsheet. A number shown as 600,0000 means Windows still uses the decimal comma.

Other classics: a decimal comma in the Windows regional settings; running from the MODSIM program folder instead of a work area; forgetting fly-outs (the exercise asks for them on every stream); using a distribution-curve model with an assumed d50 when the sheet says Karra; in the GUI, typing millimetres into a field that expects metres.

Sensitivity analysis

Vary the coarse deck around 100 mm in 5 mm steps: 85, 90, 95, 100, 105, 110, 115 mm. Keep the 20 mm deck fixed. For each run, write down:

Cut (mm)Deck areas 1 / 2 (m²)Coarse / middle / fines (t/h)d80 · d50 · d20 of coarse / middle / fines
85………
90………
95………
100………
105………
110………
115………

What to expect and explain: a coarser cut sends more rock to the middle fraction (more to the crusher, fewer coarse lumps for the mill) and loads the 20 mm deck harder, so its required area grows; the coarse product gets coarser (its d20 climbs) and the middle product's d80 climbs with the aperture. A finer cut does the opposite and moves the AG feed towards the 15 % limit. Plot the three mass flows and the three d80 values against the cut size; the required areas on a second axis. The plot is the deliverable, but the sentence that explains the slope is what the seminar wants to hear.
