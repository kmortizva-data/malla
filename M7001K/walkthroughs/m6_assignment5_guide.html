Assignment 5, step by step: the soil remediation plant

What they ask

The sheet is Assignment_5.pdf (Bertil Pålsson, 2005-12-15, two pages; it still carries the old course code KGM005). A former mechanical workshop site is to be built on; a century of lathe turnings, bore chips and file flakes, partly alloyed with chromium and nickel and partly oxidised to iron hydroxides, sits in the ground. You are to simulate a simple soil remediation plant including its water recovery. Point by point:

1. The background (section 1). A remediation plant splits the soil on a wash screen or trommel into clean stone and gravel, cleans an intermediate fraction that can be sold as sand, and cleans somewhat and dewaters the fines, which go to final deposition (in Sweden at SAKAB). The economy depends on the gravel and sand being sellable (road anti-slip in winter, for instance) and on the fines being few; the plant uses huge quantities of water, so recirculation decides both the economy and the environmental permit; it stands on asphalt with drain ditches and has strict limits on water discharge and contaminant content.

2. The material (section 2.1). A two-component system: earth (density 2.6 g/cm³, susceptibility −6.19⋅10−9 m³/kg) and iron with iron hydroxide (average density 5.0 g/cm³, susceptibility 326⋅10−9 m³/kg). Iron grade by fraction: 1.0 % above 5 mm, 5.0 % between 1 and 5 mm, 3.0 % below 1 mm. The size distribution is RRS with d63.2 = 1 mm and λ = 0.8, largest particle 50 mm; 5 % moisture; 20 t/h.

3. The process (section 2.2). Wet screening on a double-deck screen at 5 and 1 mm. The +5 mm goes straight to stockpile. The 1 to 5 mm fraction goes over a low-intensity magnetic separator; its non-magnetic product is dewatered on a dewatering screen and becomes the sand. The −1 mm fraction goes to a wet high-intensity magnetic separator, a home-built "knock-off" that delivers at most 0.5 tesla. The two magnetic products are combined into one product that leaves the circuit. The WHIMS non-magnetics and the dewatering-screen undersize go to a primary thickener (sedimentation velocity 10 mm/s); its overflow goes to a secondary thickener (1 mm/s); both underflows are combined and filtered on a band filter press. The filtrate and the secondary overflow are recycled as wash water to the screen and as dilution to the LIMS.

4. The simulation and the report (sections 2.3 and 3). Build and test-run the circuit from the data; make the assumptions you need and motivate them; suggest improvements. Report, per sub-simulation: flowsheet with solids flows, water flows, weight per cent solids and % Fe on all streams; size distributions of feed and end products; a table of model parameters and machine reports; a discussion, "especially analyses why something does not work".

Note

The course's rule 0.8 applies to the report: language help is allowed and must be declared; the assumptions, the circuit and the discussion are the group's own. This guide explains the task and how to attack it; the assumptions and the improvements are yours to make and defend.

How the work flows

This sheet gives no target number and no measured data to match. The work is build until the water circuit closes, judge the products against the plant's purpose, then improve one thing at a time, and the report is mostly the assumption list and the diagnosis. Four things have different roles:

  • Fixed for every run: the material of section 2.1 (two components with their densities and susceptibilities, the iron grade by size fraction, the RRS with d63.2 1 mm and λ 0.8, 50 mm top size, 5 % moisture, 20 t/h) and the process of section 2.2 exactly as written: double-deck wet screen, LIMS, dewatering screen, WHIMS at 0.5 T, two thickeners at 10 and 1 mm/s, band filter press, recycle of filtrate and secondary overflow to the wash water and the LIMS dilution.
  • Chosen once, and motivated: every number the sheet leaves open. Screen efficiency and wash-water rate, LIMS dilution and recovery, what a knock-off WHIMS does at 0.5 T, thickener underflow densities, filter cake moisture, the make-up water. The sheet says in bold that these must be motivated; they are criterion 1.
  • Turned in the base case: nothing in the design. The loop is convergence: the recycle must settle, the make-up must be stated, every stream must carry solids, water, % solids and % Fe. What breaks convergence is an assumption (a secondary thickener whose overflow carries solids round for ever, a wash-water rate the recycle cannot supply), and fixing it is a change to the assumption list, logged.
  • Judged, not changed: the converged base case, read against section 1: are stone and sand sellable (low iron), is the iron product carrying the contamination, are the fines few, does the water balance close with a discharge the permit would accept. What does not work goes into the discussion as it is.
  • Changed in the improvements: one machine or one flowsheet change per copy, each following from a diagnosed failure. The comparison is each copy against the base case, product by product.

In the sheet's words, "build and test run" is the base case, "assumptions that you feel necessary, these must be motivated" is the list you write before and during it, "suggestions for improvements" are the copies, and "analyses why something does not work" is the diagnosis in between. The plant's economy in section 1 is the yardstick the sheet gives instead of a number.

How to check that the plant works

  • The sheet's yardstick is section 1, not a number. Stone and sand must be sellable, so their iron grade is the first check: state the limit you use (a road anti-slip grit has no use for iron chips) and read % Fe on the stockpile and the sand. Fines must be few, so the cake tonnage as a share of the 20 t/h is the second check; it is mostly the RRS, not the machines. The iron product must carry the contamination: iron recovery to the magnetic products is the third. Water is the fourth: make-up and discharge with the secondary overflow's solids, which is the permit's number.
  • Converged means closed. The recycle of filtrate and secondary overflow must settle to steady flows; a water balance around the whole plant must give feed moisture plus make-up equal to cake moisture plus discharge plus what leaves with stone, sand and iron product. If MODSIM oscillates, the thickener overflow solids are building up: a finding about the secondary thickener, not a numerical nuisance.
  • Thickeners are sized, not guessed. Area equals the volumetric overflow divided by the settling velocity, 10 mm/s for the primary and 1 mm/s for the secondary; the underflow % solids is an assumption to state. An area that comes out absurd is a diagnosis of the fines load, not an error to round away.
  • A knock-off at 0.5 T is the built-in failure. The −1 mm iron is partly hydroxide, weakly magnetic; if the separator model takes a field strength and the susceptibilities, it will show the poor recovery; if it takes only a recovery, you must translate 0.5 T into one and say how. Either way the fines cake will carry iron the plant was meant to remove, and that is the first line of the discussion.
  • An improvement is a machine. A better recovery typed into a model proves nothing; a stronger WHIMS, a gravity unit on the 1 to 5 mm, a hydrocyclone ahead of the primary thickener, a different screen cut or less wash water are changes with a machine behind them, and each must move the product it was meant to move.

Key points

  1. Close the water first, then judge, then improve. A base case whose water balance does not close cannot be diagnosed, and an improvement without a diagnosed failure behind it is a guess. Three kinds of run, three kinds of file: the converged base case, the diagnosis on paper, one copy per improvement.
  2. The grade changes with size, and the wizard can say so. The iron is 1 % in the coarse, 5 % in the middle and 3 % in the fine fraction: this is the "grade distributions" box of the Feed Stream dialog that the Exercise 1 guide left unticked. Tick it here and enter the grade per size class; otherwise the screen products all carry the same 3 % and the LIMS has nothing to do.
  3. An RRS with λ = 0.8 is very broad. With d63.2 at 1 mm and a slope of 0.8, about a third of the material is below 0.4 mm and a good deal is slime; the fines stream is the expensive one (deposition at SAKAB), so the amount of fines is the plant's economic result before anything is separated. Compute the distribution by hand first: it is your problem definition.
  4. Two separators, two physics. The LIMS on the 1 to 5 mm takes the strongly magnetic iron particles; the 0.5 tesla WHIMS on the fines is asked to take iron hydroxides, which are only weakly magnetic. What a knock-off at 0.5 T can and cannot do is the sheet's built-in "why something does not work", and the models in MODSIM will show it only if you give them the susceptibilities and the field.
  5. The water circuit is the plant. Wash water, LIMS dilution, two thickeners with given settling velocities, a filter press and a recycle of filtrate and clear overflow: the water balance must close, the make-up water must be stated, and the thickeners must be sized from the settling velocities (area from flow over velocity) and the solids they receive. This is the "water recovery" of the task, and the permit lives on it.
  6. Assumptions are graded, improvements are graded. Everything the sheet does not give (screen efficiency, LIMS recovery of the iron, WHIMS behaviour at 0.5 T, thickener underflow densities, filter cake moisture, wash-water rates) is an assumption to be written down with a reason; and the report ends with suggestions that follow from what did not work (a stronger WHIMS, a gravity unit for the middle fraction, a hydrocyclone ahead of the primary thickener, a different screen cut).
  7. The rubric. Problem definition and assumptions; flowsheet and material definition; model selection and parameters; simulation results; discussion. This assignment weighs the first and the last more than the others.

How to start, step by step

1

Compute the feed by hand: size distribution and iron by fraction

ClickRRS: $P(d) = 1 - \exp[-(d/1\ \mathrm{mm})^{0.8}]$ at the sieve sizes you will use from 50 mm down to about 10 µm; then the mass in the three grade fractions (+5 mm, 1 to 5 mm, −1 mm) and the iron in each at 1, 5 and 3 %. Feed 20 t/h at 95 % solids (5 % moisture).
ExpectThe tonnage of stone, sand-to-be and fines, and the average Fe grade of the feed, before any machine is placed.
WhyCriterion 1; and the fines tonnage is the plant's economics in one number.
The RRS with slope 0.8 puts real mass below 10 µm; choose the bottom size class with the thickeners in mind.
2

Define the two-component material with grade by size

ClickNew project A5_soil: components earth (2.6 g/cm³) and iron (5.0 g/cm³) with the sheet's susceptibilities where the model asks for them; size classes 50 mm to about 10 µm; feed 20 t/h at 95 % solids; PSD from step 1; tick the grade-distribution box and enter 1, 5 and 3 % Fe for the three ranges.
ExpectA feed fly-out with 20 t/h, about 2.5 % Fe on average, and a Review with no errors.
WhyThe whole assignment stands on this material; the sheet's grades by fraction are its only chemistry.
Susceptibility units: the sheet gives m³/kg (mass susceptibility) in units of 10⁻⁹; check what the magnetic separator model expects.

Section 2.2 describes the plant in one paragraph. Here it is drawn, with a question mark at every place the sheet gives no number: each of those is a line of your assumption list.

3

The wet double-deck screen and the stockpile

ClickDouble-deck screen at 5 and 1 mm with wash water (state the rate); +5 mm to a stockpile product; 1 to 5 mm to the LIMS; −1 mm to the WHIMS. Choose the screen model and its efficiency (state it).
ExpectThree streams whose tonnages match step 1's fractions within the screen's imperfection.
WhySection 2.2's first sentence.
Wet screening at 1 mm carries fines with the water into the coarse products; the model's efficiency or bypass decides how much, and it is an assumption.
4

LIMS on the middle fraction, dewatering screen, sand

ClickLIMS with dilution water from the recycle (state the pulp density it needs); magnetic product to the combined iron product; non-magnetics to a dewatering screen; screen oversize = sand product, undersize to the primary thickener.
ExpectA sand product with a low Fe grade and a stated moisture; the LIMS recovery of iron as a documented parameter.
WhySection 2.2, second and third sentences.
The LIMS recovers the strongly magnetic iron; iron hydroxide in the 1 to 5 mm fraction stays in the sand unless you say otherwise, and that is worth a sentence in the discussion.
5

WHIMS on the fines at 0.5 tesla

ClickWHIMS unit on the −1 mm stream with the maximum field of 0.5 T and the two components' susceptibilities; magnetics to the combined iron product; non-magnetics to the primary thickener.
ExpectA recovery of the weakly magnetic iron hydroxide that is probably poor; write down what the model does with 0.5 T and why.
WhyThe sheet's home-built separator is the deliberate weak point of the process.
If the model has no field-strength input, you must translate 0.5 T into a recovery assumption and say so; do not let a default make the decision.
6

Thickeners from the settling velocities, then the filter

ClickPrimary thickener fed by the WHIMS non-magnetics and the dewatering undersize, sized from the settling velocity 10 mm/s (area = volumetric overflow / velocity); its overflow to the secondary thickener at 1 mm/s; both underflows (state their % solids) to the band filter press (state the cake moisture); filter cake = the fines product to deposition.
ExpectTwo thickener areas, two underflow flows, a filter cake tonnage and moisture, and a filtrate flow.
WhySection 2.2's water recovery; the fines tonnage to SAKAB is the economic result.
The secondary thickener's overflow must be clean enough to reuse; the model's overflow solids are the permit's number.
7

Close the water circuit

ClickRecycle the filtrate and the secondary overflow to the wash screen and the LIMS dilution; add a fresh make-up water stream to close the balance; run until the recycle converges; tabulate water in every stream. When it will not converge, change the one assumption that breaks it, as "How to check that the plant works" describes, and log the change.
ExpectA closed water balance with a stated make-up and discharge, and % solids on every stream: the base case, saved as A5_soil.PAK.
Why"Including its water recovery" is the task; the permit limits on discharge are the background section's point.
A recycle that does not converge usually means the thickener overflow solids build up; that is a finding about the secondary thickener, not a numerical nuisance.
8

Run, read every product, and diagnose

ClickRun the whole circuit. For the stone, the sand, the iron product and the fines cake: tonnage, % Fe, % solids, size distribution. Compare against the plant's purpose: sellable stone and sand (low Fe), few fines, an iron product that actually carries the contamination. Change nothing in this step.
ExpectA list of what works and what does not: most likely the WHIMS leaves iron hydroxide in the fines, the sand keeps some iron, and the fines are a large share of the feed.
WhyThe report asks for "analyses why something does not work".
Fe in the sand is the sellability problem; Fe in the fines is the deposition problem; keep the two apart in the discussion.
9

Suggest and test improvements

ClickOne change per copy of the project: a proper WHIMS at a higher field, a gravity unit (jig or spiral) on the 1 to 5 mm sand, a hydrocyclone ahead of the primary thickener, a different screen cut, less wash water. Compare products and fines tonnage with the base case.
ExpectTwo or three documented improvements with numbers, each following from a diagnosed failure.
WhySection 2.3's last sentence.
An improvement that only changes an assumed efficiency proves nothing; change a machine or a flowsheet.
10

Write the report from the sheet's list

ClickPer sub-simulation: the flowsheet with solids, water, % solids and % Fe on every stream; the size distributions of feed and end products; the parameter and machine-report table; the discussion of what does not work. Add the assumption list with its reasons and the AI-use declaration.
ExpectA report where every assumption is visible and every improvement has a before-and-after number.
WhyThe sheet says the assumptions "must be motivated" in bold; the marking follows the sentence.
This is the last seminar of the course; the opposition will ask about the water balance first.

Deliverables checklist

Common mistakes

  • Improving before diagnosing, or tuning an assumption until the products look good: the base case is meant to show what does not work, and the report is graded on saying why.
  • One Fe grade for the whole feed; the grade-by-size box is the difference between a plant and a mixer.
  • An RRS with λ = 0.8 cut off at 0.1 mm, so that the fines disappear from the balance and the deposition tonnage looks fine.
  • A WHIMS that recovers iron hydroxide at 0.5 T because a default said so.
  • No fresh make-up water and an "open" recycle; the water balance then means nothing.
  • Thickener areas guessed instead of computed from the settling velocities.
  • Improvements that are efficiency numbers, not machines.
