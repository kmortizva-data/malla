What they ask

The sheet is Assignment_5.pdf (Bertil Pålsson, 2005-12-15, two pages; it still carries the old course code KGM005). A former mechanical workshop site is to be built on; a century of lathe turnings, bore chips and file flakes, partly alloyed with chromium and nickel and partly oxidised to iron hydroxides, sits in the ground. You are to simulate a simple soil remediation plant including its water recovery. Point by point:

1. The background (section 1). A remediation plant splits the soil on a wash screen or trommel into clean stone and gravel, cleans an intermediate fraction that can be sold as sand, and cleans somewhat and dewaters the fines, which go to final deposition (in Sweden at SAKAB). The economy depends on the gravel and sand being sellable (road anti-slip in winter, for instance) and on the fines being few; the plant uses huge quantities of water, so recirculation decides both the economy and the environmental permit; it stands on asphalt with drain ditches and has strict limits on water discharge and contaminant content.

2. The material (section 2.1). A two-component system: earth (density 2.6 g/cm³, susceptibility −6.19⋅10−9 m³/kg) and iron with iron hydroxide (average density 5.0 g/cm³, susceptibility 326⋅10−9 m³/kg). Iron grade by fraction: 1.0 % above 5 mm, 5.0 % between 1 and 5 mm, 3.0 % below 1 mm. The size distribution is RRS with d63.2 = 1 mm and λ = 0.8, largest particle 50 mm; 5 % moisture; 20 t/h.

3. The process (section 2.2). Wet screening on a double-deck screen at 5 and 1 mm. The +5 mm goes straight to stockpile. The 1 to 5 mm fraction goes over a low-intensity magnetic separator; its non-magnetic product is dewatered on a dewatering screen and becomes the sand. The −1 mm fraction goes to a wet high-intensity magnetic separator, a home-built "knock-off" that delivers at most 0.5 tesla. The two magnetic products are combined into one product that leaves the circuit. The WHIMS non-magnetics and the dewatering-screen undersize go to a primary thickener (sedimentation velocity 10 mm/s); its overflow goes to a secondary thickener (1 mm/s); both underflows are combined and filtered on a band filter press. The filtrate and the secondary overflow are recycled as wash water to the screen and as dilution to the LIMS.

4. The simulation and the report (sections 2.3 and 3). Build and test-run the circuit from the data; make the assumptions you need and motivate them; suggest improvements. Report, per sub-simulation: flowsheet with solids flows, water flows, weight per cent solids and % Fe on all streams; size distributions of feed and end products; a table of model parameters and machine reports; a discussion, "especially analyses why something does not work".

How the work flows

This sheet gives no target number and no measured data to match. The work is build until the water circuit closes, judge the products against the plant's purpose, then improve one thing at a time, and the report is mostly the assumption list and the diagnosis. Four things have different roles:

  • Fixed for every run: the material of section 2.1 (two components with their densities and susceptibilities, the iron grade by size fraction, the RRS with d63.2 1 mm and λ 0.8, 50 mm top size, 5 % moisture, 20 t/h) and the process of section 2.2 exactly as written: double-deck wet screen, LIMS, dewatering screen, WHIMS at 0.5 T, two thickeners at 10 and 1 mm/s, band filter press, recycle of filtrate and secondary overflow to the wash water and the LIMS dilution.
  • Chosen once, and motivated: every number the sheet leaves open. Screen efficiency and wash-water rate, LIMS dilution and recovery, what a knock-off WHIMS does at 0.5 T, thickener underflow densities, filter cake moisture, the make-up water. The sheet says in bold that these must be motivated; they are criterion 1.
  • Turned in the base case: nothing in the design. The loop is convergence: the recycle must settle, the make-up must be stated, every stream must carry solids, water, % solids and % Fe. What breaks convergence is an assumption (a secondary thickener whose overflow carries solids round for ever, a wash-water rate the recycle cannot supply), and fixing it is a change to the assumption list, logged.
  • Judged, not changed: the converged base case, read against section 1: are stone and sand sellable (low iron), is the iron product carrying the contamination, are the fines few, does the water balance close with a discharge the permit would accept. What does not work goes into the discussion as it is.
  • Changed in the improvements: one machine or one flowsheet change per copy, each following from a diagnosed failure. The comparison is each copy against the base case, product by product.

In the sheet's words, "build and test run" is the base case, "assumptions that you feel necessary, these must be motivated" is the list you write before and during it, "suggestions for improvements" are the copies, and "analyses why something does not work" is the diagnosis in between. The plant's economy in section 1 is the yardstick the sheet gives instead of a number.

How to check that the plant works

  • The sheet's yardstick is section 1, not a number. Stone and sand must be sellable, so their iron grade is the first check. State the limit you use: a sensible one is 1.0 % Fe, the grade of the stone as dug, which the sheet itself calls relatively clean. Fines must be few, so the cake tonnage as a share of the 20 t/h is the second check; it is mostly the RRS, not the machines. The iron product must carry the contamination: iron recovery to the magnetic products is the third. Water is the fourth: make-up and discharge with the secondary overflow's solids, which is the permit's number.
  • Converged means closed. The recycle of filtrate and secondary overflow must settle to steady flows, and a water balance around the whole plant must give feed moisture plus make-up equal to cake moisture plus discharge plus what leaves with stone, sand and iron product. A run that says "converged" can still be wrong here, because the loop loses water only through the products and creeps towards its answer. On this engine the Bounded Wegstein method stopped after 12 iterations at a state where the water balance was 52 % out; direct substitution with a tolerance of 10−5 and up to 999 iterations settled in 92 and repeated the open-loop results within 0.4 %. So check the balance yourself, not only the word "converged".
  • Thickeners are sized, not guessed, and twice. The clarification area is the overflow flow divided by the settling velocity (Metso 6:3), 10 mm/s for the primary and 1 mm/s for the secondary. The thickening area is the feed solids divided by the most flux the settling curve can carry (King 6.3), which KYNC prints in its report. Take the larger, round the diameter up to 0.5 m, and read the report's loading, which is the required flux over the maximum. With the course's settling curve the primary is decided by thickening, not by clarification.
  • The knock-off at 0.5 T is the failure the sheet expects, so test it before you write it. DOFI caps its recovery between 5 and 95 %, and the sheet's iron, with a susceptibility of 326×10−9 m³/kg, reaches the 95 % ceiling in every size class at 0.5 T. On this engine the field only starts to matter below about 0.3 T (94.2 % at 0.3 T, 89.8 % at 0.1 T), so a 2 T separator would give the same product. The model does not fail here; a real knock-off may, and only a test on the soil would say how much: a magnetic tube test splits the ferromagnetic chips from the paramagnetic hydroxide, and a laboratory high-intensity test at 0.5 T shows what the hydroxide does. That is the honest line for the discussion.
  • An improvement is a machine. A better recovery typed into a model proves nothing; a magnet on the stone, a hydrocyclone for fine sand, a bigger primary thickener or a different screen cut are changes with a machine behind them, and each must move the product it was meant to move.

Key points

  1. Close the water first, then judge, then improve. A base case whose water balance does not close cannot be diagnosed, and an improvement without a diagnosed failure behind it is a guess. Three kinds of run, three kinds of file: the converged base case, the diagnosis on paper, one copy per improvement.
  2. The grade changes with size, and the wizard can say so. The iron is 1 % in the coarse, 5 % in the middle and 3 % in the fine fraction: this is the "grade distributions" box of the Feed Stream dialog that the Exercise 1 guide left unticked. Tick it here and enter the grade per size class; otherwise the screen products all carry the same 3 % and the LIMS has nothing to do.
  3. An RRS with λ = 0.8 is very broad. With d63.2 at 1 mm and a slope of 0.8, 63 % of the soil is finer than 1 mm, 38 % is finer than 0.4 mm and about 10 % is finer than 63 µm, the silt. The fines stream is the expensive one (deposition at SAKAB), so the amount of fines is the plant's economic result before anything is separated. Compute the distribution by hand first: it is your problem definition.
  4. Two separators, two physics, and two models that do not show it. The LIMS on the 1 to 5 mm takes the strongly magnetic iron particles; the 0.5 tesla WHIMS on the fines is asked to take iron hydroxides, which are only weakly magnetic. The sheet builds its "why something does not work" on that, but the models blur it: WDMS never reads the susceptibility, and DOFI already recovers 95 % of this iron at 0.5 T. Say what the models do, and say what a real plant would do differently.
  5. The water circuit is the plant. Wash water, LIMS dilution, two thickeners with given settling velocities, a filter press and a recycle of filtrate and clear overflow: the water balance must close, the make-up water must be stated, and the thickeners must be sized twice, for clarification (area from flow over velocity) and for thickening (area from the solids over the flux the pulp can carry). This is the "water recovery" of the task, and the permit lives on it.
  6. Assumptions are graded, improvements are graded. Everything the sheet does not give (screen efficiency, wash-water rate, LIMS dilution, WHIMS behaviour at 0.5 T, the settling curve, filter cake moisture) is an assumption to be written down with a reason and, where one exists, a source: Metso's handbook gives the wash water, the LIMS pulp density and the thickener unit areas. The report ends with suggestions that follow from what did not work.
  7. The rubric. Problem definition and assumptions; flowsheet and material definition; model selection and parameters; simulation results; discussion. This assignment weighs the first and the last more than the others.

3

The wet double-deck screen and the stockpile

ClickDouble Deck Screen unit at 5 and 1 mm with 20 m³/h of wash water, with the model DSC1 filled as the fold below says; +5 mm to a stockpile product; 1 to 5 mm to the LIMS; −1 mm to the WHIMS.
ExpectThree streams whose tonnages are step 1's fractions plus the screen's imperfection: with E₁ 0.9 and E₂ 0.85 the stone comes out near 2.8 t/h instead of 0.53, because a tenth of everything finer lands on it.
WhySection 2.2's first sentence.
DSC1 spreads its inefficiency over every finer size class, slimes included, so the stone is mostly misplaced sand and fines; a real wet screen with sprays would wash more of them through. Say so in the assumption list.

The fields below are the ones the models window shows, read from the GUI's own catalogue, which matches every window captured so far, field for field. The sheet gives few machine data, so most values are starting points to state in the assumption list, and each fold says which.

Double Deck Screen → DSC1, the wet double-deck screen: eleven fields
  • Which model. The Double Deck Screen unit offers DSC1 (its default), with an efficiency you choose for each deck, and DSC2, Karra's model, which works the efficiency out from the deck's size and load (Assignment 1's guide fills DSC2 field by field). The sheet asks you to state the screen's behaviour, and DSC1's efficiencies are exactly that statement, so start with DSC1 and keep DSC2 as a check of the deck size.
  • Top deck mesh (h₁) = 0.005 m and Lower deck mesh (h₂) = 0.001 m: the 5 and 1 mm of section 2.2, in metres.
  • Top deck efficiency (E₁) = 0.9 and Lower deck efficiency (E₂) = 0.85 to start. The share of the undersize that really passes each deck. Wet screening at 1 mm is harder than at 5 mm, which is why the lower deck starts lower. Both are assumptions to state.
  • What DSC1 does with them, measured on the engine. The efficiency is flat: with E₁ = 0.9, 10 % of every size class below 5 mm stays on the top deck, from 4 mm grains down to the slimes, and with E₂ = 0.85, 13.5 % of every class below 1 mm ends in the sand. The engine also places each size class at its mean size, so each deck really cuts at about 0.84 of its mesh, and the class that holds 5 mm stays with the stone.
  • Surface water on top oversize = 5 % and Surface water on lower oversize = 20 %: the moisture of each product, in per cent. The stone leaves as wet as the soil arrived; the sand's value only sets how much water reaches the LIMS mixer, where the dilution water tops it up to 40 % solids anyway.
  • Specify screen dimensions = off, so Top deck length, Lower deck length and Screen width are not read. With the box on, the model compares the deck with the area the load needs, as in Assignment 1; the sheet gives no screen size.
  • Screens in parallel = 1.
4

LIMS on the middle fraction, dewatering screen, sand

ClickWet Drum Mag Separator (the LIMS, model WDMS) with dilution water to 40 % solids (Metso's handbook, section 5:26: a concurrent LIMS normally runs at 35 to 45 % solids); magnetic product to the combined iron product; non-magnetics to a Dewatering Screen (model DWSC); screen oversize = sand product, undersize to the primary thickener. Both folds below give their fields.
ExpectA sand product with a low Fe grade (on this engine 7.2 t/h at 0.09 % Fe) and a moisture near 20 %.
WhySection 2.2, second and third sentences.
In reality a LIMS picks the strongly magnetic iron and leaves most iron hydroxide in the sand; the sheet makes both one component, so WDMS cannot tell them apart, and that is worth a sentence in the discussion.
Wet Drum Mag Separator → WDMS, the LIMS: three fields
  • Which model. The Wet Drum Mag Separator unit offers WDMS (its default) and WDM2, a five-field variant. WDMS has no field for the magnet's strength and never reads a susceptibility: it sends a particle to the non-magnetics by how much of the first component (earth, in this guide's order) it holds. A particle of pure earth goes to the non-magnetics; a particle of pure iron goes to the magnetics, except for a by-pass share that slips into the non-magnetics anyway.
  • Exponent for recovery curve (n) = 2.5: the default. How steeply the recovery rises with the earth content of a particle. With the two components as free particles, every particle is either pure iron or pure earth, so it hardly matters here; say so.
  • By-pass fraction (α₀) = 0.2 and Exponent for effect of size on bypass (β) = 1.5: the defaults. Measured on the engine, the by-pass of a size class is α=α0e−βd/1mm, with d the upper edge of the class: 20 % for the finest particles, about 4 % at 1 mm and almost nothing above 2 mm. So on the sand the LIMS takes practically all the iron, and the sand passes with room to spare; doubling the by-pass to 0.4 still leaves it at 0.18 % Fe.
  • The water. WDMS sends 20 % of its feed water with the magnetics, which is why the iron product leaves wet.
Dewatering Screen → DWSC, the sand's dewatering screen: eight fields
  • Which model. The Dewatering Screen unit offers DWSC (its default, Ng's model of a vibrating dewatering screen) and SCR1, a simple screen. DWSC works out how much water the sand keeps from the screen's size and its vibration, and it carries fines through the mesh with the water, which is what the sand product needs.
  • Mesh size (a) = 0.0005 m. The sand starts at about 0.8 mm (where the lower deck really cuts), so a 0.5 mm mesh holds all of it; a finer mesh keeps more of the misplaced fines in the sand. State your choice.
  • Ultimate moisture content (Mu) = 7.5 %: the default. The moisture the sand would reach on a very long screen; the sand's real moisture comes out above it (19.7 % on this engine).
  • Length of screen (L) = 3.5 m, Width of screen (W) = 1 m, Angle of inclination (θ) = 15°, Vibration frequency (f) = 300 rpm, Vibration amplitude (A) = 0.02 m and Angle of vibration relative to screen (ξ) = 45°: the defaults. They describe the model's example machine. The sheet gives none of them, so keep them and read the report: DWSC prints its load as a percentage of the capacity it recommends. The sand screen runs at about a third of it; a screen above 100 % needs to be wider.
  • Outlets. The water and the fines through the mesh leave by the undersize, which goes to the primary thickener; the dewatered sand is the product.
5

WHIMS on the fines at 0.5 tesla

ClickWHIMS unit on the −1 mm stream with the maximum field of 0.5 T and the two components' susceptibilities; magnetics to the combined iron product; non-magnetics to the primary thickener.
ExpectThe sheet expects a poor recovery, but DOFI takes 95 % of this iron at 0.5 T, its ceiling, and sends 5 % of the earth along, its floor. Write down what the model does with 0.5 T and why it cannot show the weakness the sheet has in mind.
WhyThe sheet's home-built separator is the deliberate weak point of the process.
The WHIMS model does have a field for the field strength, so type the 0.5 T there; do not leave its default of 2 T, which is four times what the home-built separator delivers. Then run a short field sweep (0.02 to 2 T) to show where the field starts to matter.
WHIMS → DOFI, the home-built WHIMS: eight fields
  • Which model. The WHIMS unit has one model, DOFI (Dobby and Finch). It captures a particle on the magnetised matrix when the magnetic force wins over the drag, so it reads each component's susceptibility from the Components tab together with the field strength.
  • Its limits, measured on the engine. DOFI never recovers less than 5 % or more than 95 % of a component in a size class. With the sheet's susceptibility the iron sits at the 95 % ceiling from 0.5 T up, and 5 % of the earth always follows it, which dilutes the iron product. The recovery falls only below about 0.3 T: 94.2 % at 0.3 T, 89.8 % at 0.1 T, 67.5 % at 0.02 T.
  • Magnetic field strength (B) = 0.5 T: the sheet's maximum for the home-built separator. The default is 2 T.
  • Matrix saturation Ms (Ms) = 1 T, Interstitial velocity (U) = 0.2 m/s and Matrix loading fraction (Lm) = 0.8: the defaults. The magnetisation of the steel matrix, the speed of the pulp through it and how full the matrix may get. The sheet describes none of them, so state them.
  • M50 (small-particle branch) (M50,s) = 0.0127 and M50 (large-particle branch) (M50,l) = 0.0127: the defaults. Calibration values of the capture curve, one for fine and one for coarse particles; without test data they stay as they are.
  • Residual water on mags (w) = 10 %: the default, the water the magnetic product carries.
  • Units in parallel (n) = 1.
6

Thickeners from the settling velocities, then the filter

ClickPrimary thickener fed by the WHIMS non-magnetics and the dewatering undersize; its overflow to the secondary thickener; both underflows to the band filter press (state the cake moisture); filter cake = the fines product to deposition. Thickener units with the model KYNC and a Filter unit with FILT, filled as the folds below say. Size each thickener twice, as "How to check that the plant works" says, and keep the larger diameter.
ExpectTwo thickener diameters with their loading below 1, two underflow flows, a filter cake tonnage and moisture, and a filtrate flow. On this engine the primary comes out at 5 m (decided by thickening, loading 0.95) and the secondary at 1 m (decided by clarification).
WhySection 2.2's water recovery; the fines tonnage to SAKAB is the economic result.
A primary sized to the edge of its capacity leaves no margin: one metre smaller, it spills fines over the secondary, and with the loop closed they go back to the screen and into the products. Say how much margin you keep, for example Metso's unit area for tailings (section 6:3, from 5 m² per t/h of solids).
Thickener → KYNC, the two thickeners: four fields
  • Which model. The Thickener unit offers THIC (its default), KYNC and ADOR. THIC has one field, Underflow solids (Cu), and sends every solid to the underflow at any size, so it can never be overloaded. KYNC works out how much solid the thickener can carry down from Kynch's settling theory, so an undersized thickener lets fines over, and it takes a settling velocity, which is what the sheet gives. In Exercise 15, KYNC at 10 m sent slime to the overflow and 11.3 m brought it under 2 % solids.
  • What KYNC does below its capacity, measured on the engine. It sends every solid to the underflow and does not classify, so the secondary thickener receives clear water until the primary spills. And it discharges at Kynch's conjugate concentration, so a thickener near its capacity gives a thin underflow: the base-case primary at 95 % of its capacity discharges at only 25.6 % solids, and the band press recovers most of the water. King (section 6.3) says why: the ideal Kynch thickener has no compression zone, and a real underflow comes out denser.
  • Thickener diameter (D): the larger of the clarification diameter (overflow flow divided by the settling velocity) and the thickening diameter (feed solids divided by the maximum flux in the KYNC report), rounded up to 0.5 m. One value for each thickener.
  • Terminal floc settling velocity (Vt) = 0.01 m/s for the primary and 0.001 m/s for the secondary: the sheet's 10 and 1 mm/s, in metres per second.
  • Number of W-N terms = 2 and W-N α/β pairs [α1,β1…α5,β5] = the course's pairs of Exercise 15: α 0.01 with β 1.5, and α 5×10−11 with β 5. These Wilhelm-Naide pairs shape how the settling slows down as the pulp thickens; the sheet gives none, so borrow the course's and declare it. On this GUI's engine, Exercise 15 only matched the course's solution with every α multiplied by 1000 to the power of its β (316.2 and 5×104), and with those the primary's maximum flux at 10 mm/s is 0.134 kg/m²·s. Whether the GUI passes the α as typed is still to be checked, so compare your thickener report with the Exercise 15 folder before you trust the overflow.
Filter → FILT, the band filter press: one field
  • Which model. The Filter unit has one model, FILT: every solid goes to the cake, and the water the cake does not hold goes to the filtrate.
  • Solids in filter cake (Cs) = 75 %: the default. The sheet asks you to state the cake moisture, and this field is its complement. Exercise 15's drum filter makes a cake at 85 % solids (15 % moisture); a band press on fine soil holds more water, so the default is a fair start. It moves the make-up water a lot: on this engine a cake at 65 % needs 8.3 m³/h of fresh water and one at 85 % only 5.0, against 6.4 at 75 %.
7

Close the water circuit

ClickFirst the open loop: wash and dilution stay as water feeds, the filtrate and the secondary overflow leave as a "recycle" product, and the make-up is wash plus dilution minus recycle. That is exact as long as the recycle carries no solids. Then close it for real in a copy: the recycle and a fresh make-up stream into a water tank (a Mixer), and a splitter that feeds the wash water and the dilution, with direct substitution, a tolerance of 10−5 and up to 999 iterations (Size_Conv. fold). Tabulate water in every stream.
ExpectA closed water balance with a stated make-up and no discharge, and % solids on every stream: the base case, saved as A5_soil.mpd. On this engine the make-up is 6.4 m³/h (0.32 m³ per tonne of soil), and the closed copy repeats the open one within 0.4 %.
Why"Including its water recovery" is the task; the permit limits on discharge are the background section's point.
The loop only loses water through the products, so a solver can stop at a wrong state and still say "converged": check the balance around the plant. And if the recycle carries solids, the open loop is no longer exact, which is a finding about the primary thickener.
8

Run, read every product, and diagnose

ClickRun the whole circuit. For the stone, the sand, the iron product and the fines cake: tonnage, % Fe, % solids, size distribution. Compare against the plant's purpose: sellable stone and sand (low Fe), few fines, an iron product that actually carries the contamination. Change nothing in this step.
ExpectA list of what works and what does not. On this engine the sand passes (0.09 % Fe), the stone fails (3.3 % Fe, because it is mostly misplaced sand and fines), 45 % of the soil leaves as fines cake although the cake carries only 0.19 % Fe, and the WHIMS does not fail. Then test each verdict against its assumption, one diagnostic run at a time: a perfect screen, a weaker or stronger field, a doubled LIMS by-pass, a wetter or drier cake, a smaller primary.
WhyThe report asks for "analyses why something does not work".
Fe in the stone and the sand is the sellability problem; the tonnage of fines is the deposition problem, and it is decided by size, not by iron. Keep the two apart in the discussion.
9

Suggest and test improvements

ClickOne change per copy of the project, each answering a failure the diagnosis found. The three that worked on this engine are in the folds below: a magnet on the stone, hydrocyclones that turn the clean fines into fine sand, and a primary thickener with a margin. Compare products, fines tonnage and water with the base case, then put the ones that stay together in a final copy and close its water loop too.
ExpectTwo or three documented improvements with numbers, each following from a diagnosed failure. With all three, the plant sells about 16 t/h (82 % of the soil, against 7 t/h in the base case) and deposits about 2.4 t/h.
WhySection 2.3's last sentence.
An improvement that only changes an assumed efficiency proves nothing; change a machine or a flowsheet. And say what the diagnosis ruled out: a second LIMS (the sand already passes), a better screen (even a perfect one leaves the stone at 2.6 % Fe) and a stronger WHIMS (the model is already at its ceiling).

Each improvement below starts from the failure it answers, and each was run on the MODSIM engine as one copy of the base case. Write the rule you use to size a new machine before you run it: then the result is the rule's, not a number tuned until it looked good.

I1 · A magnet on the stone (a second WDMS)
  • The failure. The stone carries 3.3 % Fe, and 83 % of that iron comes with sand and fines the top deck did not pass. A better screen cannot fix it: even perfect decks leave 2.6 % Fe, because the grains just under 5 mm are the 5 % Fe fraction.
  • The machine. An overband or drum magnet on the stone conveyor, drawn as a second Wet Drum Mag Separator with WDMS and its three defaults (n 2.5, α₀ 0.2, β 1.5) on the top-deck oversize. Its magnetics join the iron product.
  • What it did. The stone drops to 0.18 % Fe and becomes sellable, and the iron product rises to 61.8 % Fe with 96 % of the soil's iron. The stone's grading does not change: it is still mostly misplaced sand, which only a better wet screen would fix.
Hydrocyclone → CYCL, I2, cyclones that turn the clean fines into fine sand: thirteen fields
  • The failure. 45 % of the soil leaves as fines cake at 0.19 % Fe, five times under the sellable limit: it is deposited for its size, not for its iron, and 77 % of the soil below 1 mm is coarser than 0.1 mm.
  • The rule, written before the runs. No roping by either of the checks CYCL prints (the Mular-Jull warning, King 4.7, and Concha's ratio of the air core to the spigot, which must stay at 1 or below: above 1 the air core chokes the discharge, King 4.6); a pressure drop between 50 and 150 kPa, around the 100 kPa of the course's cyclone in Assignment 2; the earth's corrected cut nearest 63 µm, the sand-silt boundary of ISO 14688-1; and as few cyclones in parallel as possible.
  • What the rule found. Every single cyclone ropes, because one spigot would have to pass almost 8 t/h of sand. A spigot of 0.116 of the diameter always chokes (Concha 1.13), and with two to four cyclones every other geometry either ropes or leaves the pressure window. With six in parallel exactly one passes: 100 mm with a spigot of 0.15.
  • Cyclone diameter (DC) = 0.1 m and Number in parallel (n) = 6: the design above.
  • Spigot (apex) diameter (×DC) (DU) = 0.15: the default 0.116 chokes by Concha's check.
  • Vortex-finder-to-spigot distance (×DC) (H) = 3, Inlet diameter (×DC) (DI) = 0.2 and Vortex-finder diameter (×DC) (DOV) = 0.167: the defaults, the standard proportions of the model.
  • Feed head (HEAD) = 3 m: the default. The pressure drop is not an input: the report prints it from the flow, 59 kPa here.
  • D50 calibration factor (F1), Sharpness calibration factor (F2) and Flow-split calibration factor (F4) = 1: Plitt's model uncalibrated, because there is no cyclone data for this soil; say so.
  • Slurry viscosity (μ) = 0.0012 Pa·s, Density-variation exponent (AN) = 0.5 and Separating-zone density fraction (ρf) = 0: the defaults.
  • What it did. The earth's corrected cut is 29 µm, finer than 63, so the fine sand carries some silt; the fine sand is 6.6 t/h at 0.16 % Fe, sellable, and the fines cake falls from 9.0 to 2.4 t/h.
The fine sand screen: a second DWSC, sized by its own report
  • Why a screen. The cyclone underflow leaves at about 50 % solids, too wet to stockpile, so it goes over a second dewatering screen; the water and the silt through its mesh go to the primary thickener.
  • Mesh size (a) = 0.0001 m: 0.1 mm, fine enough to hold the sand while the silt passes with the water.
  • Width of screen (W) = 1.2 m. The rule: 1 m, or the narrowest width in steps of 0.2 m whose report stays at 100 % or less of the recommended capacity. At 1 m the report said 109 %; at 1.2 m it says 91 %. The other fields as the sand screen.
I3 · A primary thickener with a margin
  • The failure. Sized by the rule of step 6, the primary runs at 95 % of its capacity, and a primary one metre smaller spills 2.9 t/h of fines over the secondary; with the loop closed they return to the screen, the recycle carries 9.8 t/h of solids and the iron product falls to 47.8 % Fe.
  • The machine. The same primary sized at Metso's unit area for tailings, 5 m² per t/h of solids (section 6:3), instead of the edge of its capacity: 8 m instead of 5 in the base case.
  • What it did. The loading falls from 0.95 to 0.37 and the products do not change, as they should not while a thickener is under capacity: the gain is the margin, which is what protects the water loop. In the final design the cyclones take most of the solids away, so the same rule gives a 4 m primary.

Deliverables checklist

Common mistakes

  • Improving before diagnosing, or tuning an assumption until the products look good: the base case is meant to show what does not work, and the report is graded on saying why.
  • One Fe grade for the whole feed; the grade-by-size box is the difference between a plant and a mixer. And the two size classes that hold 5 mm and 1 mm given the grade of one side only, so the feed no longer averages 3.63 % Fe.
  • An RRS with λ = 0.8 cut off at 0.1 mm, so that the fines disappear from the balance and the deposition tonnage looks fine.
  • Iron listed before earth in the Components tab: WDMS takes the first component as the non-magnetic one, so the LIMS sends the earth to the iron product.
  • Writing that the 0.5 T WHIMS fails because the sheet calls it a knock-off, without checking the model: DOFI already recovers 95 % of this iron at 0.5 T. Say what the model does, and what a test on the soil would have to show.
  • The WHIMS field strength left at the model's default of 2 T instead of the sheet's 0.5 T.
  • No fresh make-up water and an "open" recycle whose make-up nobody computed; the water balance then means nothing.
  • Trusting "converged" on the closed water loop: check the balance around the plant, because a solver can stop at a wrong state.
  • Thickener areas guessed, or computed for clarification only: with the course's settling curve the primary is decided by the solids it must carry down.
  • A primary sized to the edge of its capacity with no word about the margin: one metre less and it spills fines into the recycle.
  • A THIC thickener anywhere in the plant: it sends every solid to the underflow at any size, so it can never show an undersized thickener.
  • Expecting the stone to clean up with a better screen: DSC1's inefficiency is flat over every finer size, and even perfect decks leave the grains just under 5 mm, which carry 5 % Fe.
  • A cyclone that passes the Mular-Jull check but has a Concha ratio above 1: its air core chokes the spigot.
  • Improvements that are efficiency numbers, not machines, or new machines sized by trial until the products looked good instead of by a rule written first.
